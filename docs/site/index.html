    Ivy Vaults

    A vault’s life

    Liquidity providers (LPs) back one option trade. The buyer pays a premium for the right to trade at an agreed price, the strike. A call is the right to buy the underlying asset; a put is the right to sell it.

    Two different claims: LP premium is available after activation. The remaining pool is available only after settlement.

    People and funds

    One vault backs one option trade. A new trade needs a new vault.

    Who makes the trade

    The owner sets the terms; LPs provide collateral; the buyer pays for the option; the bid master selects and submits the winning offer.

    Follow an instruction

    The Hub checks instructions and coordinates each separate vault. Choose a role to see its route; one person can hold more than one role.

    Who calls what

      Where the tokens live

      Each vault holds its own collateral and reserves. The shared Hub coordinates the trade without holding the tokens.

      Approve the vault

      Token approvals target the individual vault, never the Hub. LPs deposit there; the buyer’s premium and any payout reserves stay there too.

      Pause new commitments

      A guardian can block new creation, deposits, auction opening and activation. Existing exercise, claims and permitted exits remain available; deadlines do not move.

      Open

      The option has not been sold. LPs can still add or withdraw collateral.

      Choose what to sell

      The owner defines the trade before deposits: tokens, exact expiry, exercise policy, settlement choices and the limits a buyer’s offer must meet.

      Set the boundaries

      The owner fixes the tokens and exact expiry, then sets the limits acceptable to LPs. A call can require a minimum strike; a put requires a maximum strike. Both can require a minimum premium.

      While Open, the owner may tighten permitted limits: raise the minimum premium, raise a call’s strike floor or lower a put’s strike ceiling. Depositors’ protections cannot be loosened; immutable fields stay fixed.

      Choose the backing

      In a WETH/USDC option, WETH is the underlying asset and USDC is the quote token used to state the strike price. LPs back a covered call with WETH or a cash-secured put with USDC.

      The premium is the buyer’s payment for the option. It can use the underlying, quote or a third token; it is accounted for separately from the backing.

      Fix how the option settles

      Physical settlement exchanges underlying and quote tokens at the strike. Cash settlement pays the option’s intrinsic value from the collateral.

      The protocol launches with cash disabled. Cash-enabled vaults may allow Cash or Either; with Either, the winning bid chooses the method. Once activated, the buyer cannot switch methods.

      LPs fund the vault

      Deposits mint shares in this vault. While Open, an LP can burn shares to withdraw collateral; opening the auction closes that exit.

      Deposit for shares

      Only the owner may deposit by default. Other LPs need public deposits enabled, and admission must not be paused. The deposit mints shares representing that LP’s stake in this vault.

      For example, contributing 2 WETH of a final 10 WETH pool gives an LP 20% of its shares. Those shares determine its portion of the net premium and, later, the remaining pool.

      Withdraw before the lock

      An LP can take some or all of its collateral back while Open. After the auction opens, this withdrawal route is unavailable unless the auction is cancelled.

      Lock the funded pool

      Opening requires enough collateral and a transaction before expiry. A scheduled start allows anyone to submit that transaction once its time arrives.

      Open the auction

      The owner may open it directly or set a public start time. Opening freezes deposits, withdrawals and the terms for this auction.

      See the auction →

      Auction

      The collateral is committed, but the option has not yet been sold.

      Buyers offer a premium

      Bidding happens off-chain. Each signed offer is tied to this vault, auction and share supply, and must fit the owner’s limits.

      Sign and fund the offer

      The buyer signs the proposed strike, premium, token pair, exercise style and settlement method for this vault and auction. The signature also binds the share supply, a nonce and a validity deadline.

      The buyer funds its wallet and approves the vault to collect the premium. The bid master submits the signature; the Hub rejects a stale or mismatched bid. Signing alone does not sell the option.

      Signed-bid format and validation ↗

      Collateral stays committed

      LPs cannot deposit or withdraw during Auction. Shares are transferable only when the administrator enables transfers.

      Activate the winning offer

      Activation validates the selected offer and collects the premium in one transaction. A failed check leaves the vault in Auction.

      Accept the winning bid

      The bid master submits the buyer’s signed offer before expiry. Success fixes the option terms and makes the buyer’s exercise right live.

      Cancel an unsold auction

      Cancellation returns the vault to Open with its collateral inside and clears the scheduled start. LPs can then withdraw, or a new auction can be opened before expiry.

      Return to Open

      The bid master can cancel at any time. The owner can cancel after the timeout, at expiry, or during an admission pause.

      Return to funding →

      Live

      The buyer has paid. The backing stays committed until the option settles.

      LPs can claim premium

      Activation sets aside the platform fee and credits the net premium to shareholders. That premium is earned independently of the eventual option outcome.

      Collect the earned payment

      LPs can claim their net premium as soon as activation succeeds. For example, a 1,000 USDC gross premium at an illustrative 2% platform fee leaves 980 USDC for LPs and 20 USDC for the treasury.

      Unclaimed entitlement follows transferred shares. Burning shares preserves any unpaid credit, and premium already claimed is never paid again. LPs keep the earned premium whether the option is exercised, expires or is unwound.

      Buyer uses the option

      American exercise may begin before expiry; European exercise starts at expiry. Partial exercise leaves the rest Live when the vault’s policy permits it.

      Know the physical deadline

      An American option can be exercised from activation; a European option must wait until expiry. Both physical styles then share the vault’s additional exercise window.

      Physical exercise must execute strictly before expiry plus that window. At the deadline it is refused, and anyone can submit expiration to settle the unused rights. No market-price report is needed.

      Exchange tokens physically

      For a 10 WETH call at a 3,000 USDC strike, full physical exercise means the buyer pays 30,000 USDC and receives 10 WETH. For the matching put, the buyer delivers 10 WETH and receives 30,000 USDC.

      If partial exercise is enabled, exercising 2 of the 10 WETH leaves the other 8 Live. Exercising all remaining notional settles the vault. The premium is a separate payment and does not change with exercise.

      After full exercise →

      Delegate execution if needed

      The buyer may name an executor to submit exercise or payout claims, and a recipient to receive assets. For physical exercise, the caller supplies the payment tokens.

      Cash uses a reported price

      Cash settlement pays intrinsic value in the collateral token. It needs an authorized price report and cash-enabled terms; the feature is disabled by default.

      Use the price for this moment

      Before expiry, American cash exercise uses a fresh observation from an authorized publisher. From expiry onward, cash exercise and expiration use one final price for the vault’s original expiry.

      Cash has no physical-style grace cutoff: exercise can continue until the vault settles. Anyone may submit expiration from expiry once the final price exists. Expiration reserves the buyer’s payoff; it does not pay it automatically.

      See who keeps the collateral

      With no earlier exercise, a cash call backed by 10 WETH, strike 3,000 USDC and final price 3,300 USDC reserves 10 × (3,300 − 3,000) / 3,300 ≈ 0.9091 WETH for the buyer. About 9.0909 WETH remains for LPs.

      With no earlier exercise, a cash put on 10 WETH at a 3,000 strike, with a final price of 2,700, pays 3,000 USDC from its 30,000 USDC backing. The other 27,000 USDC remains for LPs. An out-of-the-money option has zero payoff; premium stays earned.

      If the cash price is missing

      Cash exercise and expiration need the applicable valid report. Without it, the collateral remains committed until publication recovers or the parties execute an agreed unwind.

      Missing prices and recovery ↗

      Close the remaining position

      A Live vault reaches Settled through full exercise, an expiration transaction, or an executed agreement between the buyer and every current shareholder.

      Submit expiration

      Anyone may expire a physical option once its exercise window has closed. Cash expiration is available from expiry with a final price. Time passing alone never settles a vault.

      See settlement and claims →

      Agree to end early

      The buyer signs the refund, deadline and current option snapshot before the owner or buyer submits the proposal. Every current shareholder then approves. Replacing a proposal needs a new buyer signature. Each LP separately supplies its required share of the refund in premium tokens; the refund does not claw back earned premium or platform fees.

      Execution must still match the approved shares and exercised amount. Share transfers invalidate affected approvals; funding requirements must be checked again. Further exercise invalidates the agreed snapshot. When a valid agreement executes, the vault settles and the buyer’s refund is reserved for claim.

      Agreement, funding and recovery ↗

      After the agreement executes →

      Settled

      The option has ended. Remaining assets stay in the vault until claimed.

      LPs collect the remainder

      Current shareholders burn shares to claim their proportional part of the unreserved pool. Buyer obligations and unpaid premium stay protected in separate reserves.

      Redeem the remaining pool

      A shareholder burns some or all of its shares for the same proportion of the unreserved assets. Holding 20% of the remaining shares means receiving 20% of the remaining pool.

      If a call expired without exercise, that pool is still its underlying collateral. After full physical exercise it holds the quote tokens the buyer paid. Partial exercise can leave both. Premium and buyer reserves are excluded; claims do not consume someone else’s payment.

      Buyer collects reserved assets

      Cash expiration may reserve a payoff, and an executed unwind may reserve a refund. These are separate from assets already delivered during exercise.

      Claim the reserved payout

      The buyer or its executor claims the reserve for the configured recipient. A zero payoff creates nothing to claim; LP residual claims cannot consume a buyer reserve.
