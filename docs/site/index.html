Protocol guide

Ivy Vaults

Deposit assets to back one option, earn its premium, and claim what remains when the option ends.

An Ivy vault is a smart contract that holds assets to back a single option sold to a buyer. The buyer pays for the rights that the option gives them, and the people who provide those assets earn that payment. What they receive at the end also depends on the option's terms and whether the buyer uses those rights. Each vault handles one trade; selling another option requires a new vault.

The option deal

An option gives its buyer a right they can use under agreed conditions, without requiring them to use it. The seller receives an upfront payment, called the premium, for taking on the corresponding obligation.

CallThe buyer has the right to buy the asset at an agreed price.
PutThe buyer has the right to sell the asset at an agreed price.

Who takes part

Vault owner

Creates the vault and chooses the terms it is willing to offer. The owner can provide the assets alone or allow other depositors to join.

Liquidity provider LP

Deposits assets to back the option and receives shares recording their interest in the vault. A shareholder is whoever currently owns those shares, including someone who received them from an earlier depositor.

Market maker Buyer

Buys the option and offers a price through a bid, which is a signed proposal for the trade.

Bid master

The authorized auction operator who selects a bid outside the blockchain and submits it to the contracts.

Accepting that bid on-chain is called activation. The contracts check its terms, collect the buyer's premium and make the option active.

The buyer pays the premium in the premium token. This example uses USDC for both pricing and payment, but the premium token can differ from the quote token. Each permitted combination is called a quote/premium pair.

The strike stays fixed even if the market price changes. That changing market price is called the spot price, and it determines whether using the option's right is valuable to the buyer.

What happens when the buyer uses the option?

Using the option's right is called exercise. Ivy supports two settlement types, which determine whether the parties exchange tokens at the strike or the vault pays the option's value.

Physical settlement

The parties exchange the actual tokens at the strike. A call buyer pays quote tokens and receives underlying; a put buyer delivers underlying and receives quote tokens.

Cash settlement

The vault pays the option's value from the deposited assets. Here, "cash" means a value payment, so it can be paid in underlying tokens for a call.

When can the buyer exercise?

Every option has an exact expiry timestamp and an exercise style. The style determines when the buyer may use the right; the settlement type also affects the deadline.

American

Allows exercise before expiry. American physical options also have a limited exercise window after expiry, while American cash exercise ends at expiry.

European

Waits until expiry. European physical options have a limited exercise window after expiry; European cash options settle at expiry or later without a manual exercise call.

The exact deadlines appear in the exercise-window table later in this guide.

Who submits the call, and who gets paid?

The buyer can appoint an executor to exercise and claim payouts on its behalf, and choose a recipient address that receives those assets. These are separate permissions: the executor can submit the call, but cannot redirect payment to itself unless it is also the buyer's chosen recipient.

Collateral and shares

The assets LPs deposit are the vault's collateral. They back the seller's obligation to the buyer, so earning a premium does not guarantee that LPs will get the same assets or market value back. Depositing the underlying creates a covered call; depositing the quote token creates a cash-secured put. The examples below show how those deposits determine the trade size.

collateral == underlying

Covered call

  • Deposit the underlying asset, such as WETH, to back the call. If the buyer physically exercises, the vault delivers that underlying in exchange for payment at the agreed strike.
  • Bids quote strike in a listed quote token and premium in that pair’s fixed premium token.
  • The option notional equals the underlying deposited, so a vault holding 10 WETH can sell a call covering 10 WETH.
collateral != underlying

Cash-secured put

  • Deposit the quote token, such as USDC, to fund the put. The underlying asset that the buyer can deliver during physical exercise is fixed when the vault is created.
  • A put vault accepts one quote/premium pair, with the collateral itself serving as the quote token.
  • The notional is the collateral divided by the strike: 30,000 USDC at 3,000 USDC per WETH backs a put covering 10 WETH. In raw token units, the calculation is collateral × underlyingUnit / strike, where underlyingUnit = 10^underlyingDecimals is the number of smallest units in one whole underlying token.

When the option ends, shareholders make a claim to collect their portion of the remaining assets. Claiming burns the submitted shares, meaning those shares are removed from circulation. The contracts keep amounts owed as unpaid premium or buyer payments in reserves. The remaining, unreserved assets form the settlement pool available to shareholders.

Shares
The vault mints one share for each smallest unit of collateral it actually receives. The IvyShares contract issues them using ERC-1155, a token standard that lets one contract track shares for multiple vaults under separate token IDs. Each token ID matches its vault ID. Shares can be transferred in every phase, carrying the right to claim a proportion of the remaining pool after settlement. Premium already earned at activation belongs to the holder who earned it, even if those shares later change hands.
Kind
The collateral and underlying addresses determine the option kind at creation. Matching addresses mean a covered call; different addresses mean a cash-secured put.
Approvals
Before a contract can collect tokens from a wallet, the wallet gives it a token allowance, commonly called an approval. This approval must target the individual vault address, because that contract pulls and holds the assets. The hub is the shared contract that coordinates vault operations, but it does not take custody of the tokens. You can deposit through the hub or call deposit(amount) directly on the vault; both routes follow the same deposit rules.
Premium
The buyer pays the premium when the winning bid is activated. The people holding shares at that moment are the activation holders, and they can immediately collect their earned amount through claimPremium(vaultId). They retain this claim after transferring or burning their shares. The vault sets aside unpaid premium so it cannot be spent on other claims. Small amounts left by rounding each holder's entitlement down, called rounding dust, stay set aside permanently.

Vault lifecycle

Each vault moves through four phases, starting with collateral collection and ending with final claims. Opening the auction fixes the collateral available for bidding; activation then commits the vault to the winning option. The phase determines which actions are available, as described below. Finishing the trade is called finalization: the vault enters Settled, where holders can collect their claims but no new option can begin.

Settlement outcomes

Settlement completes the option and determines which assets each side can collect. What an LP receives depends on the option kind, how much the buyer exercises, and whether settlement is physical or cash. Physical exercise exchanges underlying and quote tokens at the strike, so the remaining pool can contain both assets. Cash settlement pays the buyer from the collateral instead. Cash settlement at expiry uses a finalized expiry price: a historical price signed by Ivy and published to the price feed, the contract that checks and stores price reports. Once published, that price cannot be replaced. It represents the agreed expiry price even if settlement takes place later. The formulas below include earned premium, although holders claim that premium separately from their share of the settlement pool.

Vault terms

The earlier sections describe the trade in plain language. This reference maps those choices to the contract fields an owner configures before LPs commit collateral. An option is in the money when exercise has positive value to the buyer: spot is above the strike for a call, or below it for a put. Some fields below use basis points, abbreviated bps, where 100 bps equals 1%. While the vault remains Open, the owner can tighten the permitted option terms in the directions shown below, such as raising the minimum premium or narrowing the allowed settlement types. The auction schedule can also change, while fields marked immutable stay fixed. Activation checks the winning bid against the current terms and reverts if any required check fails.

Bidding and exercise

A market maker participates as the option buyer by signing a bid for a particular auction and collateral amount. The signature also binds a hash of the accepted token pair, a compact identifier for that exact configuration, along with expiry and execution settings, so the bid master cannot substitute different terms when activating it. Cancelling and reopening an auction changes its ID and requires a new signed bid.

For the unwind agreement introduced in the lifecycle section, consent must reflect current ownership. A share transfer invalidates the sender's and recipient's approvals, and those holders must approve again at their new balances. Holders can revoke approval before execution. Replacing the proposal or exercising more of the option requires fresh agreement on the updated state; proposing an unwind does not suspend ordinary exercise or settlement.

  1. Sign the bidThe buyer signs structured trade data using EIP-712, a signing format that binds the message to a particular chain and contract. The signature uses the IvyVaultsHub domain, version 2, the chain ID and the deployed hub address. The buyer must hold MARKET_MAKER_ROLE for activation. Each bid includes a buyer-chosen identifier called a nonce. Activating a bid consumes that nonce so it cannot be used again, and the buyer can invalidate an unused nonce with cancelBid(nonce). Every bid also has a validUntil deadline.
  2. Approve the vaultThe buyer approves the vault to collect the full premium, calculated as floor(premium × notional / underlyingUnit). This approval funds activation only. If the option is physically exercised later, the buyer or executor making that call must separately approve and supply the required payment assets.
  3. ActivationThe bid master submits the signed bid, and the vault collects the premium in that same transaction. If the signature, terms or premium funding fail the contract checks, activation reverts and the vault does not become Live.
  4. ExerciseWhere manual exercise is supported, the buyer or its appointed executor can exercise part or all of the remaining notional within the permitted window. Cash payouts come from the vault's collateral. Only the buyer can change the executor or payout recipient, and exercise assets always go to that configured recipient. 
struct Bid {
  uint256 vaultId;
  address marketMaker;
  address quoteToken;
  uint256 strike;       // quote per 1 underlying
  uint256 premium;      // premiumToken per 1 underlying
  uint8   style;        // 0 European, 1 American
  uint8   settlement;   // 0 Physical, 1 Cash
  uint64  expiry;       // unix seconds
  uint64  validUntil;   // signature deadline
  uint256 nonce;
  uint256 auctionId;
  uint256 collateralAmount;
  bytes32 pairHash;
  address executor;
  address recipient;
}

For raw token amounts, underlyingUnit = 10 ** underlyingDecimals. Strike and premium are raw payment-token units per whole underlying.

Expiry reports are final. Cash settlement uses Ivy’s signed 30-minute average ending at the exact expiry. The feed verifies the signature, not the averaging calculation. Reports are write-once and support multisig contract signers. If Ivy cannot publish, cash vaults remain locked until a valid report arrives or the buyer and all current shareholders consent to an unwind. There is no stale-price or zero-payout fallback.

Contracts and entrypoints

The hub coordinates the vault lifecycle, while each vault holds its own tokens and reserves. Separate contracts track transferable shares, premium entitlements, unwind consent and signed prices. The responsibilities below explain which contract owns each part of the process before the entrypoint reference lists the calls available in each phase.

Immutable hubIvyVaultsHub

Creates vaults, verifies bids and runs the phase machine. Linked libraries implement term rules and settlement logic. Token-moving hub entrypoints use nonReentrant.

EIP-1167 cloneIvyVault

Each vault is an EIP-1167 clone that holds its own tokens and tracks reserves separately for each token. Ordinary transfers requested by the hub can spend only unreserved assets, while premium payments must also stay within an independent fixed budget.

ERC-1155IvyShares

This contract issues the LP shares, with minting and burning restricted to the hub. Before a balance changes, it notifies the premium and unwind modules so they can preserve activation entitlements and invalidate affected votes before receiver callbacks run.

Immutable signerIvyPriceFeed

The feed provides separate lookups for fresh signed spot prices and finalized expiry prices. Prices are expressed in the quote token's smallest units per whole underlying token. Once an expiry price is finalized, it remains available for settlement even if settlement happens much later.

Activation incomeIvyPremiums

Lazy checkpoints preserve premium ownership. Payments can only start through the guarded hub claim; the vault enforces the premium budget independently.

Shareholder consentIvyUnwind

One active agreement per vault. Transfers invalidate affected votes, replacement supersedes approvals, and normal exercise or settlement remains available.

Linked rules libraryIvyVaultRules

Creation validation and term tightening. Creation checks admission. Tightening checks the owner and Open phase.

Linked settlement libraryIvyOptionSettlement

Exercise, cash settlement and residual claims execute against hub storage, under its reentrancy guard. Library addresses are fixed in hub bytecode.

Calls by phaseRoles · DEFAULT_ADMIN · GUARDIAN · BID_MASTER · MARKET_MAKER

Two libraries deploy first, followed by the vault implementation, hub, shares, premium module, unwind module and signed feed. Addresses are predicted from a fixed nonce sequence; deployment and recovery verify constructor bindings and every embedded library link. There are no upgrade, library or module replacement setters. Admins retain role, metadata and future-default controls; guardians can pause admission.
