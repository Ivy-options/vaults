Protocol guide

Ivy Vaults

Deposit assets to back one option, earn its premium, and claim what remains when the option ends.

An Ivy vault is a smart contract that holds assets to back a single option sold to a buyer. The buyer pays for the option's rights, and the people who provide the assets earn that payment. Their final claim depends on the option's terms and whether the buyer exercises. Each vault handles one trade; selling another option requires a new vault.

The option deal

An option gives its buyer a right they can use under agreed conditions, without requiring them to use it. The seller receives an upfront payment, called the premium, for taking on the corresponding obligation.

CallThe buyer has the right to buy the asset at an agreed price.
PutThe buyer has the right to sell the asset at an agreed price.

Who takes part

Vault owner

Creates the vault and chooses the terms they are willing to offer. The owner can provide the assets alone or allow other depositors to join.

Liquidity provider LP

Deposits assets to back the option and receives shares recording their interest in the vault. A shareholder is whoever currently owns those shares, including someone who received them from an earlier depositor.

Market maker Buyer

Buys the option and offers a price through a bid, which is a signed proposal for the trade.

Bid master

The authorized auction operator who selects a bid outside the blockchain and submits it to the contracts.

Accepting that bid on-chain is called activation. The contracts check its terms, collect the buyer's premium and make the option active.

The buyer pays the premium in the premium token. This example uses USDC for both pricing and payment, but the premium token can differ from the quote token. Each permitted combination is called a quote/premium pair.

The strike stays fixed even if the market price changes. The current market price, or spot price, determines whether exercise has value to the buyer.

What happens when the buyer uses the option?

Using the option's right is called exercise. With physical delivery, the parties exchange the actual tokens at the agreed strike. A call buyer pays quote tokens and receives underlying; a put buyer delivers underlying and receives quote tokens. The agreed strike fixes this exchange; physical exercise and expiration need no settlement-price report.

When can the buyer exercise?

Every option has an exact expiry timestamp and an exercise style. The style determines when the buyer may use the right. Physical delivery includes a limited exercise window after expiry.

American

Allows exercise while Live before expiry and during the limited exercise window afterward.

European

Waits until expiry, then allows exercise during the limited exercise window. The buyer cannot exercise before expiry.

The exact deadlines appear in the exercise-window table later in this guide.

Who submits the call, and who gets paid?

The buyer can appoint an executor to exercise and claim payouts on its behalf, and choose a recipient address that receives those assets. These are separate permissions: the executor can submit the call, but cannot redirect payment to itself unless it is also the buyer's chosen recipient. Only the buyer can change the executor or recipient.

Vault lifecycle

Each vault moves through four phases, from collateral collection to final claims. Opening the auction fixes the collateral available for bidding; activation commits the vault to the winning option. Each phase permits the actions listed below. Finalization ends the trade and moves the vault to Settled, where holders can collect their claims but the vault cannot sell another option.

Unwind: ending early by agreement

An unwind ends a Live option by agreement. The owner or buyer can propose it, but execution requires the buyer’s signature and approval from every current shareholder, covering 100% of the outstanding shares. The proposal records the exercised notional, share supply and refund, along with a nonce and future deadline. Execution must occur no later than that deadline, while the vault is still Live.

Consent must reflect current ownership and the amount already exercised. A nonzero transfer to another holder invalidates the sender’s and recipient’s approvals; they must approve again at their new balances. Holders can revoke approval. Replacing the proposal or exercising more of the option requires fresh agreement. A proposal does not suspend ordinary exercise or expiration.

If a refund is agreed, each current LP funds their proportional contribution separately in the premium token. Each LP must approve the vault clone before funding. The minimum contribution is rounded up to a raw token unit. The executor provides no tokens. Zero refund is also allowed. The buyer or executor collects a funded refund for the configured recipient with claimPayout; shareholders claim the remaining pool. Earned premium and completed exercises are preserved. Holders separately use claimPremium for unpaid premium, including after burning all their shares.

  • Zero refund: propose zero, obtain all current-holder approvals and the buyer signature for that nonce, then execute without refund funding. Holders claim the remaining pool and unpaid premium separately.
  • 100 USDC refund: when premium uses six-decimal USDC, set refund to 100000000. With a 60/40 share split, the LPs fund 60 and 40 USDC; the buyer or executor claims it separately after finalization.
  • Expired or replaced proposal: while Live, propose again with a future deadline. The new nonce requires fresh approvals and buyer signature. Old consent cannot execute the replacement; the position keeps its normal exercise and expiration deadlines.

Follow the unwind runbook and commands. Auction cancellation is a separate pre-activation action: no premium has been collected.

Premium and fees are earned at activation. Refunds are negotiated and funded separately. Claiming premium does not waive the current LP’s contribution. Before execution, each funder can recover their deposit; withdrawing revokes their current consent. After execution, only excess contributions and apportioned ceiling surplus are recoverable. Recovery stays tied to the funder after share transfers or burns. The final participant recovering ceiling surplus receives the rounding dust; the buyer receives exactly the agreed amount. See the unwind runbook for the formula.

The emergency pause applies only to admission. A full execution freeze would require a separate specification for permissions, affected actions, deadlines, resumption and outstanding payment obligations. See the operating policy.

Platform fees

The buyer pays gross premium at activation. The vault deducts floor(grossPremium × feeBps / 10,000) in the premium token and allocates the remainder to LPs, who can claim immediately. For 1,000 USDC at 200 bps (2%), the treasury receives 20 USDC and LPs receive 980 USDC. Integer rounding is down to the smallest token unit, leaving any fractional fee unit in the net LP allocation.

The global rate starts at zero. Accounts with PLATFORM_FEE_MANAGER_ROLE set it, up to 10,000 bps. The deployment admin initially holds the role and administers its membership. Each vault records the current global rate as its maximum at creation. Activation uses the latest global rate and rejects a rate above that cap. Lower the rate or create a new vault. minPremium remains a gross threshold.

The deployment admin is the initial treasury recipient. Accounts with DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE can change it for future activations. Activation records the applied rate and treasury. Anyone can trigger claimPlatformFee() on the vault to pay the recorded treasury once. Later treasury or rate changes do not alter existing fees.

Fee reserves are separate from LP premium and buyer obligations, including when token addresses overlap. Fees remain earned after exercise, expiry or unwind; negotiated refunds require separate funding. The calculator below assumes zero fees. See fee administration and commands.

What each side receives

Finalization closes the vault and makes its remaining, unreserved assets available to shareholders. Earned premium is claimed separately.

A covered call starts with underlying tokens, such as WETH. A cash-secured put starts with quote tokens, such as USDC. The strike is the agreed price per underlying token. The table below describes the whole vault's remaining pool; each shareholder claims their proportional share.

Optional cash settlement

Cash settlement pays the option’s intrinsic value from collateral instead of exchanging the full underlying and quote amounts at the strike. It is implemented for future use and disabled by default. The physical delivery flow above works without a settlement publisher. The following rules apply to cash positions only after the administrator enables the feature.

Enable the feature and assign responsibility

  1. Arrange publication. Agree the price methodology, evidence and responsible operator. The admin can choose an EOA, a multisig or an authenticated helper contract.
  2. Grant the publisher role. Grant SETTLEMENT_PRICE_PUBLISHER_ROLE on the Hub to that address. An EOA submits price transactions directly; an optional helper submits through the same APIs. No settlement feed contract is required, and the Hub never reads prices back from a helper.
  3. Enable cash admissions. The admin calls setCashSettlementEnabled(true). New vaults may then permit Cash or Either, with a positive maxSettlementPriceAge, and bids may select Cash.

Role membership and the feature flag are independent. Granting or revoking a role never changes the flag; the on-chain setter does not check publisher coverage. The operator command checks a nominated publisher’s current role before preparing the transaction, but the admin remains responsible for continued publication. See the cash enablement runbook.

Disabling affects new positions. With the flag off, new Cash and Either vaults are rejected and cash bids cannot activate, including bids in pending auctions. Physical bids can still activate, including in existing Either vaults. Existing physical vaults keep their delivery method. Publication, exercise, expiration, claims and agreed unwind for existing cash positions remain available. Turning the feature off does not remove their payment obligations.

Each vault has its own settlement prices

Every cash vault needs its own price-setting transactions, identified by vaultId. The Hub derives the underlying, selected quote token and fixed expiry from the Live cash position and stores prices for that vault. Even matching pairs and expiries need independent reports. Any holder of the publisher role can publish for any eligible cash vault; the role does not assign a separate publisher to each vault or require a quorum.

Before expiry
American cash exercise uses that vault’s latest observation from publishExercisePrice(vaultId, price, observedAt, validUntil). It must be positive, no older than the vault’s maximum age and still within its validity deadline. New observations require strictly later observation timestamps.
At or after expiry
Cash exercise and expiration use the write-once final price from publishExpiry(vaultId, price, validUntil), submitted at or after the vault’s fixed expiry. At the exact expiry timestamp, even a fresh exercise observation is no longer sufficient. Finalized prices remain usable indefinitely.

Prices use quote-token units per whole underlying. The optional indicative priceFeed only constrains activation; it cannot authorize a cash payment. There is no indicative-price or other-vault fallback. Operators calculate the proposed 30-minute average ending at the exact expiry off-chain. Production sources, sampling and calculation rules must be approved before enabling cash; the Hub checks authority and time bindings, not market-data accuracy.

Final means irreversible. Each expiry price can be published only once, with no correction or deletion. Revoking a publisher does not erase its reports or reverse completed payments. Review the calculation and the vault ID before submitting it.

Cash exercise, expiration and claims

The buyer or executor calls exercise(vaultId, amount) to pay intrinsic value directly to the configured recipient. The fixed partial-exercise policy still applies; full exercise finalizes the vault. A zero-value exercise reverts. At or after expiry, anyone can instead call expire(vaultId) to finalize the remaining notional and reserve any buyer payout for claimPayout(vaultId). A zero expiry payout can still finalize the vault. Shareholders claim the unreserved remainder, with earned premium claimed separately as in physical delivery.

Expiration requires an on-chain transaction and that vault’s final expiry price. Cash has no physical-delivery grace window: the buyer’s remaining value is reserved on expiration instead of lapsing.

Cash payouts and worked examples

“Cash” means a value payment, not necessarily quote tokens. A call pays in underlying collateral; a put pays in quote collateral. For remaining notional N, strike K and the applicable reported price S, the whole-token formulas are shown below. On-chain buyer payouts round down.

Call: deposit 10 WETH

Buyer payout = N × max(S − K, 0) / S in WETH.

At a strike of 3,000 USDC per WETH and a finalized expiry price of 3,300, the option value is 10 × (3,300 − 3,000) = 3,000 USDC. Converting at that price pays the buyer approximately 0.9091 WETH; the pool keeps approximately 9.0909 WETH.

At or below the strike, the expiry payout is zero and the pool keeps its remaining WETH.

Put: deposit 30,000 USDC

Buyer payout = N × max(K − S, 0) in USDC.

At a strike of 3,000 USDC per WETH, the deposit covers 10 WETH. A finalized expiry price of 2,700 pays the buyer 10 × (3,000 − 2,700) = 3,000 USDC; the pool keeps 27,000 USDC.

At or above the strike, the expiry payout is zero and the pool keeps its remaining USDC.

These examples assume no earlier exercise, exclude separately earned premium and assume zero platform fees. Expiry outcomes use the finalized price for that vault’s original expiry, even when the transaction is processed later.

If a report is missing

A missing, stale or expired observation blocks early cash exercise. A missing final report blocks cash exercise at or after expiry and expiration. The remaining obligation stays locked; the contract does not treat the missing price as zero or release the buyer’s backing to LPs. Earned-premium claims remain available.

The admin can rotate publishers and disable new cash admissions while a replacement supplies each missing vault report. A late expiry report must reconstruct the approved price for the original expiry, not use today’s market price. Alternatively, the parties can agree to an unwind with unanimous current-shareholder approval, the buyer’s signature and separate refund funding. Neither the feature flag nor an admission pause blocks this recovery or changes deadlines and earned premium.

See the pricing specification for the complete rules and the publication runbook for report preparation and recovery.

Versions and existing vaults

Each Ivy deployment has fixed code and contract bindings. A new release can add features while existing vaults continue through their original Hub. Releasing new code does not migrate deposits or change the code controlling an older position.

Starting a new vault

The frontend can look up the recommended release through IvyVaultsRegistry, a separately deployed contract with a permanent discovery address. The user selects that release and sends the creation transaction directly to its Hub.

Returning to an existing vault

The position keeps its original chain, Hub address and vault ID. Exercise, expiration and claims continue through that Hub, even after another release becomes recommended. Vault IDs are local to a Hub; two releases can both have a vault numbered 1.

The registry administrator can add a release and change the recommendation. Existing registrations cannot be replaced or deleted, and the registry has no authority over vault funds. Changing the recommendation does not pause an older Hub or prevent direct access to it. Registration identifies a release; it does not prove that the release has been audited.

Fixed code also means a defect in a deployed vault cannot be patched in place. Its original settlement and agreed-unwind rules continue to apply. The administrative powers already described in this guide, including admission pauses, share-transfer controls and optional cash-price publication, remain part of each deployment. Contracts and entrypoints

The hub coordinates the vault lifecycle, while each vault holds its own tokens and reserves. Separate contracts track transferable shares, premium entitlements and unwind consent. The Hub also stores the optional cash feature’s authorized prices.

Immutable hubIvyVaultsHub

Creates vaults, verifies bids and runs the phase machine. Linked libraries implement term rules and settlement logic. Token-moving hub entrypoints use nonReentrant.

EIP-1167 cloneIvyVault

Each vault is an EIP-1167 clone that holds its own tokens and tracks reserves separately for each token. Ordinary transfers requested by the hub can spend only unreserved assets, while premium payments must also stay within an independent fixed budget.

ERC-1155IvyShares

Issues LP shares. Only the hub can mint or burn them. Before a balance changes, it notifies the premium and unwind modules so they can preserve activation entitlements and invalidate affected votes before receiver callbacks run.

Indicative price signerIvyPriceFeed

Signed spot observations constrain activation when enabled. Its legacy expiry API remains available, but new cash vaults use the Hub’s stored reports for every payment.

Settlement publisher roleHub price registry

An authorized EOA or optional helper submits exercise observations and final expiry prices for each vault ID. Prices use quote-token units per whole underlying. The Hub admin manages publishers and a separate cash feature flag, which starts disabled. Any one publisher can publish. A multisig can enforce its own signing threshold. See pricing and availability.

Activation incomeIvyPremiums

Lazy checkpoints preserve premium ownership. Only the guarded hub claim can initiate payments; the vault enforces the premium budget independently.

Shareholder consentIvyUnwind

Tracks the active agreement and current-shareholder approvals for ending early by agreement.

Linked rules libraryIvyVaultRules

Creation validation and term tightening. Creation checks admission. Tightening checks the owner and Open phase.

Linked settlement libraryIvyOptionSettlement

Exercise, cash settlement and residual claims execute against hub storage, under its reentrancy guard. Library addresses are fixed in hub bytecode.

Calls by phaseRoles · DEFAULT_ADMIN · GUARDIAN · BID_MASTER · MARKET_MAKER

Two libraries deploy first, followed by the vault implementation, hub, shares, premium module, unwind module and indicative feed. The Hub constructor has no settlement-publisher input and grants no publisher role. Addresses are predicted from a fixed nonce sequence; deployment and recovery verify constructor bindings and every embedded library link. There are no upgrade, library or module replacement setters. Admins retain role, metadata and future-default controls; guardians can pause admission.
