Cash settlement pricing

Cash-settled vaults pay intrinsic value out of the collateral, so someone has to tell the Hub what the price was. This document describes how that works: who may publish a price, what the Hub checks, what it refuses to do, and what happens when a report is missing or wrong. The Hub owns the prices and the publisher authority. An EOA or an optional helper contract publishes; nothing else is consulted.

Two things are deliberately left open. Which accounts publish in production, and how they compute their prices, are deployment decisions and are not encoded anywhere in the contracts.

Physical-only launch and later cash activation

A new Hub starts physical-only. Its constructor takes no publisher argument, grants no publisher role and leaves cashSettlementEnabled false. Physical vault creation, exercise, expiration and claims never touch a settlement price. The indicative activation feed is a separate, optional thing.

setCashSettlementEnabled(bool) flips the flag. Only DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE can call it, and it emits CashSettlementEnabledUpdated(bool enabled). Publisher membership is ordinary AccessControl under SETTLEMENT_PRICE_PUBLISHER_ROLE, with zero-address grants rejected. The two are independent: granting, revoking or renouncing the role never changes the flag, and the flag setter does not look at who holds the role.

While the flag is false, the Hub rejects creation of Cash and Either vaults and rejects cash bid activation, including in auctions that were already open. Physical bids still activate, including in existing Either vaults. A physical-only vault can never be loosened to cash. Deposits, tightening and auction cancellation follow their usual rules regardless of the flag.

The flag gates new positions and nothing else. Disabling it does not block publication, exercise, expiration, claims or agreed unwind for live cash positions. Stored observations keep their freshness limits and finalized prices stay usable. Revoking a publisher blocks that publisher's future submissions and leaves its past reports and the flag alone. A missing report keeps the obligation locked until an authorized publisher supplies it. Nothing about flags or roles converts a settlement type or discards an obligation.

Before enabling cash, the admin should have an approved methodology and a publisher with the role. The CLI's enable command checks that a nominated address holds the role, but that is a preflight for the operator's benefit, not something the contract stores or enforces. Roles can change after the check. The administrator stays responsible for coverage. Disabling needs no nomination.

Payment authority and routing

Both American and European cash options exist. Indicative activation pricing and authoritative settlement pricing are kept apart on purpose. An indicative price may constrain the winning strike; it can never authorize a payment.

Action Required price
Activation with indicative checks enabled Fresh priceFeed.spot(underlying, quote) under the activation rules
American cash exercise strictly before expiry That vault's fresh, unexpired exercise observation
American or European cash exercise at or after expiry That vault's finalized price for its fixed expiry
Cash expiration The same vault-specific final price, for all remaining notional
Physical exercise or expiration Nothing

There is no fallback from an authoritative report to the indicative feed. The exact expiry timestamp is the boundary: at that second, even a still-fresh exercise observation can no longer pay an exercise. Authorization, the partial-exercise policy, the intrinsic-value arithmetic and the payout token are the same as for any other exercise.

Public interface

The Hub stores one exercise observation and one final expiry value per vault ID. The linked settlement library reads that storage directly. There are no shared pair keys, no external settlement source and no Hub self-call. The same numeric vault ID on another Hub is unrelated storage.

bytes32 public constant SETTLEMENT_PRICE_PUBLISHER_ROLE;
bool public cashSettlementEnabled;

function setCashSettlementEnabled(bool enabled) external;

function publishExercisePrice(
    uint256 vaultId, uint256 price,
    uint64 observedAt, uint64 validUntil
) external;

function exercisePrice(uint256 vaultId)
    external view returns (uint256 price, uint256 observedAt, uint256 validUntil);

function publishExpiry(uint256 vaultId, uint256 price, uint64 validUntil) external;

function settlementPrice(uint256 vaultId)
    external view returns (uint256 price);

Both publication functions require the caller to hold the publisher role and the vault to be Live with Cash as its activated settlement type. Unknown, pre-activation, physical and finalized positions are rejected. The Hub derives the underlying, selected quote and expiry from the vault's own state rather than trusting the publisher to supply them. Prices must be positive. Events carry the vault ID and the derived pair and expiry. The read functions reject unknown vault IDs; a known vault with no observation returns zeros, and a missing final price reverts.

Vaults with identical underlying, quote and expiry still need their own price-setting transactions, and they may receive different prices. Publishing or finalizing one vault never populates, replaces or blocks another. This lets each vault be approved independently. It does not claim that different prices reflect one market methodology.

Vault terms have no settlement feed field. maxSettlementPriceAge must be positive for any vault that permits cash settlement and cannot be changed by tightening. priceFeed and maxPriceAge keep their separate activation-check meaning. A physical-only vault may use zero for the settlement age and omit the indicative feed.

Interchangeable publishers

An EOA with the role submits either publication transaction directly, naming the vault ID. A helper contract can hold the same role and call the same functions through the minimal write-side interface. The helper may compute prices under its own approved policy; the Hub never reads from it and never stores it as a dependency of any vault.

The repository ships an access-controlled example helper with tests for both EOA and contract publication. A helper must not let unprivileged callers publish through it. No helper is deployed by default. Once cash is enabled, granting an EOA the role is enough to publish. There is no feed or helper to deploy.

Exercise observations

The Hub stores the latest (price, observedAt, validUntil) per vault. Publication requires 0 < observedAt <= block.timestamp <= validUntil and a strictly increasing observedAt for that vault. Expired submissions and non-increasing observations are rejected, including an equal timestamp with a different price. Different vaults may share an observation timestamp.

When an exercise consumes the observation, the Hub requires a positive stored price, a non-future observation, block.timestamp - observedAt <= maxSettlementPriceAge and block.timestamp <= validUntil. Both bounds are inclusive and both are checked at exercise time, not at publication. No other vault's report counts. An observation published after expiry cannot stand in for the final expiry price.

A new observation affects only exercises that come after it. It cannot change a payment that already happened. Publishers should pick validity windows that fit the vault's freshness limit, because a generous validity deadline does not bypass the age check.

Exact expiry and finality

Final prices are keyed by vault ID, and the Hub takes the expiry from the vault's activated state. Publication requires block.timestamp >= expiry and is accepted through validUntil inclusive. Once a positive price is stored, every later publication for that vault is rejected, even one with the same value. Another vault needs its own finalization. Final prices stay readable forever, including after settlement; the submission deadline does not expire a stored value.

Finality is immediate and write-once, with no challenge period. That is a trust choice, and it is intentional. Review the calculation before sending the transaction, because there is no administrative correction, no deletion and no retroactive change to payouts already made. A mistaken final report is the contractual price. Incident response cannot promise an on-chain reversal.

Late publication is allowed for as long as the cash position is Live, without an arbitrary cutoff. Reconstruct the approved calculation for the vault's original expiry and submit it with a current deadline. Never substitute a current market price, another vault's report or a different expiry.

Price methodology and operational approval

Prices are integer quote-token units per whole underlying token. For WETH/USDC, 2,500 USDC per WETH is 2500000000. This is not the premium-token unit and not a generic 18-decimal convention.

The proposed expiry method is a 30-minute average ending at the exact expiry. A production methodology also has to name the data sources, the sampling frequency, weighting, rounding, the missing-sample policy and what happens when a source is down. None of that is approved yet, and deployment tooling must not invent it. Exercise observations likewise need an approved current-price source with a deterministic calculation, timestamp and validity policy. The contracts attest who published. They do not prove the source was accurate or compute either method.

Before enabling production cash admissions, record the approved methodology, the responsible operators, where the evidence lives and the freshness configuration. Tie each vault's preparation and publication requests to the methodology artifact and keep the calculation evidence per vault. Vaults may share a method and still need separate publications. This is an operational prerequisite, not an on-chain registry. Physical-only deployments need none of it, and passing synthetic tests approves nothing.

Governance, rotation and trust

The Hub admin controls the flag and the publisher role. In production that admin should be a multisig. Publishers may be EOAs, multisigs or authenticated helpers. Any role holder can publish for any eligible cash vault; there is no per-vault publisher list and no on-chain quorum. If you want a signing threshold, put it inside a publisher multisig.

Granting a replacement and revoking the old publisher is the whole recovery procedure, and it changes no vault terms. Revocation stops future publication transactions, including queued ones that land afterwards. Because settlement uses transactions rather than signed messages, there are no outstanding signatures to invalidate. Ordinary chain and destination binding applies; a transaction on another chain or Hub does not touch this Hub's state.

Stored exercise observations survive revocation and remain usable until their age or validity limit passes or a newer observation replaces them. Stored final prices survive permanently. Revocation is not a retroactive veto over data. Publisher identity is mutable, so depositors are trusting the Hub administrator and every authorized publisher for the life of the position. Losing every admin key ends governance recovery. Losing every publisher while the admin still works is fixed by rotation.

Failure and incident procedure

A missing, stale or invalid exercise observation makes early cash exercise revert. A missing final expiry report makes cash exercise at or after expiry and cash expiration revert. Neither case settles the vault at zero, advances exercised notional, releases collateral to LPs or discards the buyer's obligation. Earned-premium claims continue as normal.

If publication coverage is lost, disable new cash admissions if appropriate and rotate authority. The replacement publishes each missing vault's report. Recovery publication and settlement of existing positions work while the flag is false. Obligations stay locked until the reports arrive. Admission pause likewise leaves publication, exercise, expiration, claims and agreed unwind available and moves no deadline. Unwind still requires unanimous consent and separately funded refunds; it is not an automatic price fallback.

A wrong exercise observation is handled by publishing a strictly later, verified observation; exercises already completed stay final. A wrong finalized expiry report cannot be corrected by anyone in this design. Record the incident and stop new affected admissions if appropriate. Any negotiated remedy happens off-chain and cannot seize existing claimant balances.

Deployment and tooling notes

The per-vault publication and read APIs and the price storage layout belong to this build. They require a fresh immutable deployment with matching artifacts, libraries and helpers. Existing deployments keep their behaviour; there is no in-place upgrade or migration of live positions. The Hub constructor still has seven arguments and none of them concern settlement.

A default deployment contains no publisher, no automatic enablement and no cash methodology. Indicative signer configuration is separate, and methodology metadata in the deployment plan is optional. Plans use manifest version 6 and obsolete plans are rejected. Deployment recovery verifies immutable bindings and Hub administration without assuming any particular flag or publisher state.

The CLI has set-cash-settlement-enabled, which takes a boolean enabled and, when enabling, a nonzero publisher that already holds the role. Disabling needs no publisher. The command previews the current and requested flag and the checked publisher; the contract receives only the boolean. Role commands stay separate and never imply enablement.

Publication requests name hub, vaultId and settlementMethodology, plus a report with price, validUntil and, for exercise observations, observedAt. The preview shows the derived underlying, quote, expiry, units and vault ID. These request shapes are distinct from the indicative spot typed reports. The default examples are physical; the cash examples walk through grant, explicit enable, creation, activation and then one publication per vault. The local rehearsal test covers physical delivery with cash disabled, granting a publisher without enabling cash, enabling through the CLI, per-vault publication, disabling the flag, and recovery, expiration and claims for existing cash positions, along with unauthorized submissions, helper authentication and stale-plan rejection.

What production still needs

The implementation as shipped supports American exercise, direct role publication, immediate irreversible expiry finality, shared mutable publisher authority, survival of stored observations across revocation and locked-obligation recovery. It does not come with named admin or operator accounts, and it does not come with an approved per-pair methodology or freshness configuration. Passing the local tests does not authorize a production deployment or a production price.
