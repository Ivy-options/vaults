IVY · PROTOCOL ATLAS

Ivy Vaults

Deposit assets to back one option, earn its premium, and claim what remains when the option ends.

An Ivy vault is a smart contract for a single option trade. The buyer pays an upfront price, called the premium, for the right to trade an agreed asset at an agreed price, called the strike. Using that right is called exercising the option. The depositors provide the assets that back the trade, so what they receive at the end depends on whether the buyer exercises. Selling another option requires a new vault.

For example, an option on WETH might have a strike of 3,000 USDC per WETH. WETH is the underlying token, the asset the option is written on. USDC is the quote token, the currency used to express its price. A call gives the buyer the right to buy WETH at the strike; a put gives it the right to sell WETH at the strike.

With physical settlement, exercising exchanges those tokens. With cash settlement, the buyer receives the calculated payoff from the assets deposited in the vault instead. The guide follows the physical trade first, then explains the optional cash method.

Section 01

The people around a vault

An option trade has a seller and a buyer. In Ivy, liquidity providers, or LPs, supply the seller's backing assets, called collateral, and receive shares that record their stake in the vault. A market maker buys the option. The owner manages the vault, and the bid master selects the market maker's offer. The protocol administrator manages global settings and permissions, a different role from owning an individual vault. The roles below show who makes each decision and which contract holds the money.

OWNER

Sets the terms

The owner creates the vault and sets its tokens and trading terms. The owner may also be an LP and can fund the vault alone, accept deposits from other LPs, or do both.

LIQUIDITY PROVIDER

Supplies collateral

The LP deposits collateral and receives shares in one vault. Collateral is the asset set aside to meet the option's payment obligations. Shares record the LP's stake in that vault and determine its share of the premium and remaining assets.

MARKET MAKER

Buys and manages the option

The market maker is the option buyer and manages the position through settlement. It proposes the premium and option terms in a signed bid within the owner's limits. At activation, it pays the premium and holds the right to exercise under the agreed terms.

BID MASTER

Selects & submits

The bid master runs the auction off-chain, selects a buyer's signed bid and submits it to the Hub for activation.

Only the buyer can update the executor and recipient. An executor cannot change where assets are sent.

Section 02

Vault lifecycle

A vault moves from Open to Auction, then Live and Settled. Cancelling an auction is the only way to return to an earlier phase. Once a bid activates, the vault can only move forward to Settled.

Every vault has a fixed expiry date and time set at creation. The exercise window is the period when the buyer may use its option. Its boundaries depend on the agreed rules, so expiry does not always mean exercise must stop immediately. The physical timing rules and cash timing rules below give the exact boundaries.

Who can act in each phase
PHASE 0
Open

No option has been sold yet. This is the stage for funding the vault and preparing the terms offered to buyers.

  • LPsTake funds back.An LP can return some or all of its shares to withdraw the corresponding collateral. The contract destroys those shares, called burning, because they no longer represent a stake in the vault. This withdrawal route is available only while Open.Add funds.A deposit adds collateral and gives the depositor shares. Deposits must not be paused. Anyone other than the owner can deposit only if the vault was created with publicDeposits enabled.
  • OwnerKeep or strengthen LP protections.The owner can tighten the permitted terms while Open. For example, it can raise the minimum premium a buyer must offer, but cannot lower it after LPs have committed their funds.Manage ownership.The owner can hand management of the vault to another address.

Moving to Auction. The owner can open the auction manually with openAuction or set a scheduled start time, auctionStartsAt. Once that nonzero time is reached, anyone can submit the opening transaction. A schedule does not submit it automatically.

Auction opening must not be paused. The vault needs a nonzero collateral balance that meets the configured minimum, minCollateral, and expiry must still be in the future.

PHASE 1
Auction

The auction is open, and the trade terms are locked. The owner cannot change them while buyers are bidding.

  • LPsThe committed collateral stays fixed during the auction, so LPs cannot deposit or withdraw. If the admin enables transfers, shares remain transferable even though collateral cannot leave through a withdrawal.
  • Bid masterCompare offers off-chain and select a buyer's signed bid. The offer must fit the vault's terms, and the buyer must have enough funds and token allowance to pay the premium.

Moving to Live. The bid master calls activate(vaultId, bid, signature). If the checks pass, the buyer pays the premium and the option begins.

When can the auction be cancelled? The bid master can cancel at any time. By default, the owner has to wait until the auction has been open for three days. This waiting period is called the auction timeout. The protocol admin can choose a different waiting period for new vaults; each vault copies the current setting at creation and keeps it. The clock starts when the auction opens, not when the vault is created.

The owner can skip that wait if new deposits and auctions have been paused for this vault or across the protocol, or if the option's fixed expiry time has been reached.

What happens after cancellation? The vault returns to Open. Collateral stays in the vault until LPs choose to withdraw it; cancellation does not send funds back automatically. Deposits are possible again if they are not paused and the vault's deposit rules allow them.

The scheduled auction start is cleared. Before expiry, the owner can schedule another auction or open one manually. If expiry has already passed, no new auction can open, but LPs can still withdraw. No premium or platform fee was collected during the cancelled auction, so neither needs to be refunded.

PHASE 2
Live

The option has been sold. Collateral is now committed to the buyer's remaining rights, so LPs must wait for settlement to claim the remaining collateral and proceeds.

  • Market makerThe buyer or its executor calls exercise(vaultId, amount) during the allowed exercise window. The amount is the quantity of the underlying token, such as WETH, that is covered by that exercise. It can be part or all of the remaining option size, depending on whether the vault's terms permit exercising in smaller portions.
  • LPsPremium claims are available immediately. Shares can be transferred when enabled by the admin.

Moving to Settled. Full exercise settles the vault in the exercise transaction. Otherwise, anyone can call expire(vaultId) once expiration is allowed. Physical options must first finish their exercise window; cash options require their final expiry price.

The parties can also end the option through an unwind signed by the buyer and approved by every current shareholder.

PHASE 3
Settled

The option is over, and no further exercise is possible. Final claims may contain a different mix of tokens from the original deposit, depending on how the option ended.

  • ShareholdersCall claim(vaultId, shares) to exchange shares for a proportional part of the remaining collateral and exercise proceeds. For example, a holder of 10% of the remaining shares can claim 10% of that available pool, subject to token rounding. The shares are burned when claimed. Reserved premium, platform fees and buyer payouts are excluded from this pool. A reserve is an amount set aside for a specific payment, so it is not available for this shareholder claim.
  • Market makerThe buyer or its executor calls claimPayout(vaultId) if a payout is waiting in the buyer reserve. This can be a separately funded unwind refund or an owed cash-expiry payout for a cash-settled vault. The claim sends those assets to the configured recipient. Physical exercise already pays the recipient in the exercise transaction, so it creates no later buyer payout to claim.
Claims require transactions. Reaching Settled does not automatically distribute the remaining assets.
What a pause means. The guardian, an address with permission to pause new activity, can stop new commitments across the protocol or for one vault. This is called an admission pause. It blocks new creation where applicable, deposits, auction opening and activation. It does not stop withdrawals, transfers, exercise, expiration, claims or agreed unwinds that otherwise meet their normal rules. A pause does not extend any deadline.

Section 03

Premium and fees

The premium is what the buyer pays to obtain the option. Imagine a buyer pays 1,000 USDC and the platform fee is 2%. The vault sets aside 20 USDC for the treasury, and the shareholders at activation earn the remaining 980 USDC. They can claim it immediately, without waiting to see whether the buyer exercises. Move the fee slider below to see how the split changes. The treasury is the address that receives the platform fee. Rates are also shown in basis points, abbreviated bps: 100 bps is 1%, so 200 bps is 2%.

Each vault fixes its fee rate at creation using the current global rate. Later rate increases or decreases affect only new vaults, including when an existing vault has a zero fee. Activation uses that fixed rate and records the current treasury. Anyone can then call claimPlatformFee() on the individual vault contract once to pay that recorded treasury.

Contracts count tokens in their smallest units, called raw units. For a token with six decimal places, 1 USDC is stored as 1,000,000 raw units. A calculation that lands between two raw units must round to one of them. In the formulas below, floor means round down and ceil means round up.

LPs collect unpaid premium by calling claimPremium(vaultId). Transferring shares also transfers a proportional amount of the sender's unpaid premium to the new holder. Premium already collected is not paid again. If an LP burns shares to claim settlement assets, any unpaid premium remains available for that LP to claim separately.

Calculation
floor(grossPremium × feeBps / 10,000) in premium tokens. The fee rounds down to a whole raw token unit, leaving the rounding remainder for LPs. The owner's minPremium limit is the gross premium per whole underlying token, before the platform fee is deducted. The total payment scales that per-token premium by the option's size, called its notional. For example, 100 USDC per WETH over a 10 WETH option produces a 1,000 USDC gross premium.
Rate authority
The global fee rate starts at zero. An address with PLATFORM_FEE_MANAGER_ROLE can set it as high as 10,000 basis points, or 100%, for newly created vaults. Existing vaults keep their fixed rates. One basis point is 0.01%, so 200 bps means 2%. The deployment admin, the address chosen to administer the protocol when it is deployed, initially holds this permission and controls who else receives it.
Treasury authority
The deployment admin is the initial recipient. An address with DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE can change the recipient for future activations; later changes never rewrite recorded fees.
Earned payments
Once activation succeeds, exercise, expiry, a pause or an unwind does not take back the premium and fees already earned. The vault tracks fees, LP premium and buyer obligations separately, even if they are all owed in the same token. Unwind refunds are funded separately.

What each side receives

An LP's result depends on both the premium and the assets left after the option ends. With physical exercise, the buyer exchanges tokens with the vault at the agreed strike, so LPs may receive a different mix of tokens from their deposit. The LPs then claim the USDC left in the vault, alongside their separately earned premium. If the option expires without exercise, their WETH remains available to claim instead.

Bidding and exercise

The auction happens off-chain. A buyer signs an offer, then the bid master submits it to activate the option and collect the premium. Signing alone does not open a position. Once activation succeeds, the buyer or its executor can exercise under the agreed terms.

    Sign the bid

    The buyer signs an offer specifying the price, premium and option rules for this auction. EIP-712 is the structured signing format that binds those fields into one message.

Exercise and expiration

When the buyer exercises a physical option, it uses the right it bought: the caller pays one asset and the vault delivers the other at the agreed strike. If the buyer leaves some or all of that right unused, anyone can close it after the exercise window ends by submitting an expiration transaction. The two paths have different callers and payment requirements.

Physical exercise windows

American options may be exercised before expiry; European options must wait until expiry. Both physical styles remain exercisable for an additional period after expiry, called the grace window. This lasts 1 hour by default and is configured as exerciseWindow. The protocol admin chooses the setting for new vaults; each vault copies it at creation and keeps that duration. For example, with a one-hour window and an expiry of 12:00 UTC, exercise remains possible until just before 13:00 UTC. At 13:00 UTC it stops, and anyone can submit expiration. Section 06

Optional cash settlement

With cash settlement, the buyer receives the value of the option's payoff without exchanging the full underlying and quote amounts. That payoff, called intrinsic value, is calculated from the strike and a reported market price. Payment comes from the vault's collateral: a WETH-backed call pays WETH, even though the method is called cash settlement. This feature is implemented but disabled by default and requires an authorized price publisher.

Two separate admin controls

At launch, the protocol does not allow new cash-settled positions and has no authorized price publisher. The admin must separately enable new cash positions and give an address permission to submit their prices. Enabling one does not enable the other.

Allow new cash positions
Enabling cash admissions allows new vaults configured for Cash, meaning cash settlement only, or Either, meaning the buyer's bid may choose physical or cash settlement. It also allows cash bids to activate. Disabling admissions blocks those new commitments; existing Live positions still have to meet their payment obligations.
Authorize a price publisher
Granting the publisher role lets that address publish settlement prices. Revoking it stops future reports from that address; prices already published remain stored.

Before enabling cash positions, agree on the price methodology, evidence and operator. The publisher can be a regular wallet controlled by a private key, a multisig wallet requiring several signers, or a helper contract authorized to submit reports. The admin grants it SETTLEMENT_PRICE_PUBLISHER_ROLE, then calls setCashSettlementEnabled(true). Cash/Either vaults must set a positive maxSettlementPriceAge, the maximum age in seconds of a price used for exercise before expiry.

Publication route
A regular wallet submits a price transaction directly. A helper contract calls the same publication methods. In both cases, the report is written into the Hub; the Hub does not fetch the settlement price from a separate feed or helper.
Independence
Giving or removing publication permission does not enable or disable cash positions. Likewise, the contract lets the admin enable cash without checking that someone is ready to publish. The operator command checks a nominated publisher before submitting that change, but the admin remains responsible for arranging ongoing price publication.
When disabled
New Cash/Either vaults and cash bids, including pending auctions, are blocked. Physical bids may still activate, including in existing Either vaults.
Existing obligations
Physical vaults keep their delivery method. Existing cash positions retain publication, exercise, expiration, claims and agreed unwind; disabling removes none of their payment obligations.

Cash enablement procedure ↗

Which price does the vault use?

Cash settlement needs a price report for this specific vault. Before expiry, an American option uses a recent reported market price, called an exercise observation. That report records both the price and when it was observed. At expiry, settlement switches to one final price for the option's original expiry time, even if the transaction happens much later.

BEFORE EXPIRY

Latest exercise observation

publishExercisePrice(vaultId, price, observedAt, validUntil)
  • American cash exercise only.
  • The price must be positive and its validity deadline, validUntil, must not have passed. When the buyer exercises, the contract also checks how long ago the price was observed against maxSettlementPriceAge. That age check happens at exercise, not publication.
  • A replacement report must have a later observation time than the previous report for that vault.

AT OR AFTER EXPIRY

Write-once final price

publishExpiry(vaultId, price, validUntil)
  • Publish at or after the option's fixed expiry, before the publication request's validity deadline has passed.
  • Even a fresh exercise observation is no longer enough.
  • Once published, this final price can be used indefinitely. The publication deadline does not make it expire.
One vault per report
Every cash vault needs independent transactions keyed by vaultId, even with matching pairs and expiries. The Hub derives underlying, selected quote and fixed expiry from the Live position.
Publisher scope
Any authorized publisher may submit prices for any eligible cash vault. Publishers are not assigned to individual vaults, and the Hub does not require several publishers to agree before accepting a price.
Units & authority
Prices use raw quote-token units per whole underlying token. The optional priceFeed is a separate source used to check whether a bid's strike is acceptable at activation. Its price cannot replace a missing cash-settlement report, and neither can another vault's report.
Methodology
Operators calculate the proposed 30-minute average ending at exact expiry off-chain. Production sources, sampling and calculations need approval before enablement. The Hub verifies authority and time bindings, not market-data accuracy.
Irreversible
Final expiry prices cannot be corrected or deleted. Revoking a publisher erases neither its reports nor completed payments. Verify the calculation and vault ID before submission.

Cash exercise, expiration and claims

Exercising sends the buyer's payoff immediately. Expiration instead sets aside the remaining payoff in a buyer reserve for a later claim. Both paths use the reported price, and the distinction determines when tokens leave the vault.

EXERCISE

Vault → recipient

Buyer or executor calls exercise(vaultId, amount). Intrinsic value is paid immediately.

If the calculated payout is zero, exercise is rejected.

The vault's partial-exercise policy determines whether the buyer can exercise some of the remaining option or must exercise all of it. Full exercise settles the vault.

EXPIRATION

Vault → buyer reserve → recipient

At or after expiry, anyone calls expire(vaultId); buyer or executor later uses claimPayout(vaultId).

Expiration can settle the vault even when the buyer is owed nothing.

Expiration closes the remaining option and sets aside any amount owed to the buyer. Shareholders can claim the assets left after that reservation. Earned premium stays separate.

Cash expiration needs an on-chain transaction and that vault's final expiry price. There is no physical-delivery grace window; remaining buyer value is reserved instead of lapsing.

Missing report: the obligation stays locked

The contracts cannot calculate a cash payoff without a valid report. They keep the backing assets committed until publication recovers or the parties agree to unwind.

BLOCKED

Price-dependent settlement

Missing, stale or expired observations block early cash exercise. A missing final report blocks exercise at/after expiry and expiration.

STILL AVAILABLE

Earned-premium claims

A missing price is never treated as zero. Buyer backing cannot be released to LPs.

Section 08

Releases and existing vaults

Ivy deploys new versions as separate contract suites. Existing vaults keep their original code and Hub; they do not move to a new release. The release registry is a directory that recommends a Hub for new vaults. The example below shows why a new recommendation leaves an existing position on Hub A.

New creation
The frontend discovers the recommended release. The user selects a release and sends the creation transaction directly to its Hub.
Existing position
Exercise, expiration and claims use its original chain, Hub and local vault ID, regardless of the current recommendation. Vault IDs may repeat across Hubs.
Registry authority
Its administrator adds releases and changes the recommendation. Registrations cannot be replaced or deleted. The registry has no authority over vault funds.
Recommendation limits
Recommending a new release does not move, pause or change the code of an older position. Its users continue through the old Hub. A release appearing in the registry does not establish that it has been audited.
Immutable deployment
An existing vault keeps the code and Hub it was created with, so a defect cannot be fixed by replacing that code in place. Its original settlement and unwind rules still apply, along with its existing admission-pause controls, share-transfer controls and any cash-price publication requirements.

Section 09

Unwind by agreement

An unwind ends a Live option by agreement between the buyer and every current shareholder. They can negotiate a refund to the buyer, which current LPs fund separately. This is an alternative to exercising or waiting for expiration; the proposal, consent and funding must all be valid when it executes.

  1. Preview the next agreement, including its nonce, exercised amount, current share supply, refund and deadline, and obtain the buyer's signature.
  2. The owner or buyer submits the signed proposal, and every current shareholder approves. Replacing a proposal needs a new buyer signature.
  3. Current LPs separately fund any negotiated refund.
  4. Anyone can execute once the agreement is valid, while the vault is Live and no later than the proposal deadline.

In this example, LP A holds 60% of the shares and LP B holds 40%. A 100 USDC refund needs 60 USDC from A and 40 USDC from B, as well as the buyer's signature and both LPs' consent.

A 100 USDC refund is 100000000 raw units when USDC has six decimal places. The LPs fund it before execution; the caller who executes the agreement does not supply it.

What the proposal records
The proposal records how much of the option has already been exercised, the total share supply and the agreed refund. It also has a nonce identifying this agreement and a future deadline by which it must execute.
Consent changes
A shareholder can withdraw its approval. A nonzero share transfer to another holder invalidates both sender and recipient approvals. They approve again at their new balances.
New agreement
A replacement proposal or further exercise requires fresh agreement. If the proposal's deadline passes or the proposal is replaced, propose again while the vault is Live with a new future deadline. This is the proposal's deadline, separate from the option's expiry. The contract assigns the next nonce, and old signatures and approvals cannot execute it.
Funding
Each current LP approves the individual vault contract and funds a proportional contribution in the premium token, rounded up to a raw unit. Zero refund needs the same consent but no refund funding.
While the proposal is pending
Proposing an unwind does not pause exercise or expiration. The usual option rules keep applying until the agreement executes, and any exercises already completed are not reversed.

Recover refund contributions

Before execution
A funder can take back its refund contribution before the unwind executes, including after withdrawing approval, after the proposal's deadline or after replacement. Taking back a contribution to the current proposal also withdraws that funder's approval. Recovering funds from a replaced proposal does not affect approval of its replacement.
After execution
The buyer receives exactly the agreed refund. A funder can recover any contribution above its required amount, plus its allocated share of extra units left because each LP's required contribution was rounded up. Any smallest-token units left when dividing that surplus go to the last participant to recover a share of it.
Original funder
Recovery stays attached to the funder after share transfers or burns. Already-claimed premium does not excuse a current LP from funding its contribution.
