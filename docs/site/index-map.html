    Ivy Vaults

    Before the vault

    Deposit assets to back one option, earn its premium, and claim what remains when the option ends. An Ivy vault is a smart contract for a single option trade. The buyer pays an upfront premium for the right to trade an agreed asset at an agreed price, the strike. Using that right is exercising. Depositors provide the backing, so what they receive depends on whether the buyer exercises. Selling another option requires a new vault.

    For example, an option on WETH might have a strike of 3,000 USDC per WETH. WETH is the underlying token; USDC is the quote token. A call gives the buyer the right to buy WETH at the strike; a put gives the right to sell it.

    With physical settlement, exercising exchanges those tokens. With cash settlement, the buyer receives the calculated payoff from the assets deposited in the vault instead.

    Who is around a vault

    LPs supply the seller's collateral and receive shares. A market maker buys the option. The owner manages the vault, the bid master selects the offer, and the protocol administrator is a separate role.

    Sets the terms

    The owner creates the vault and sets its tokens and trading terms. The owner may also be an LP and can fund the vault alone, accept deposits from other LPs, or both.

    Supplies collateral

    The LP deposits collateral, the asset set aside to meet the option's payment obligations, and receives shares in one vault. Shares determine its part of the premium and remaining assets.

    Buys and manages the option

    The market maker is the option buyer and manages the position through settlement. It proposes the premium and terms in a signed bid within the owner's limits, pays the premium at activation, and holds the right to exercise.

    Selects and submits

    The bid master runs the auction off-chain, selects a buyer's signed bid and submits it to the Hub for activation.

    Runs the protocol settings

    The protocol administrator manages global settings and permissions. That is separate from owning a vault.

    How instructions reach a vault

    Every instruction goes through the shared Hub, which validates it and calls the individual vault. Pick a character to see the route.

    Who calls what

      How custody works

      The Hub validates instructions, collects the premium and moves the vault to Live. The individual vault holds the tokens and reserves, so token approvals target the vault's address.

      One Hub, separate vaults

      The Hub is the shared entry point. It validates and coordinates and holds no tokens. Each vault holds its own tokens and reserves.

      The Hub holds nothing

      The Hub validates and coordinates calls to separate vaults. It has no token custody.

      Approvals target the vault

      Approve the individual vault's address, never the Hub.

      pull and push

      The Hub calls pull(token, from, amount) to receive tokens and push(...) to send them.

      Two deposit routes

      Deposit calls can use the Hub or the vault directly; token approval targets the vault.

      Who may act for the buyer

      The buyer can use one wallet for the whole trade: it submits transactions and receives the assets. To split those jobs, the buyer can choose an executor, a recipient, or both.

      Executor and recipient

      An executor submits exercise and payout-claim transactions for the buyer. A recipient is the address that receives the buyer's assets.

      Executor

      An operator or bot. For physical exercise it must supply and approve the tokens that pay the vault.

      Recipient

      For example, an operator submits while the vault sends assets straight to the buyer's treasury or multisig.

      Only the buyer changes them

      The buyer uses set-execution; an executor cannot change where assets are sent. A zero executor address revokes delegation.

      A future swap adapter

      A recipient adapter could swap and route tokens, for example through NEAR. This is not implemented; setting a recipient alone does not swap.

      What a pause means

      The guardian, an address with permission to pause new activity, can stop new commitments across the protocol or for one vault. This is called an admission pause.

      Pause new commitments

      A pause blocks new commitments but lets existing positions run under their normal rules, on their normal deadlines.

      What it blocks

      New creation where applicable, deposits, auction opening and activation.

      What it never stops

      Withdrawals, transfers, exercise, expiration, claims and agreed unwinds that meet their normal rules.

      Deadlines do not move

      A pause does not extend any deadline.

      How to pause

      pause with vaultId: "0" for global admission, or a specific ID for one vault. A paused vault must be unpaused before another deposit or auction.

      Open

      No option has been sold yet. This is the stage for funding the vault and preparing the terms offered to buyers.

      What the vault holds

      I · Backing
      Deposited collateral: the vault's current balance of the chosen collateral token.
      II · Premium and fees
      Empty: no buyer payment has been collected.

      Owner creates the vault

      The owner creates the vault with a fixed expiry, the two tokens, the settlement style, and the terms buyers may bid against. Expiry never moves after this.

      Set the terms

      A call vault can require a minimum strike and a minimum premium; a buyer's offer must meet both. While Open, limits can only get stricter, and immutable fields cannot change.

      Three token roles

      Underlying, quote and premium. Collateral is whichever of the first two LPs deposit: 10 WETH for a covered call, 30,000 USDC for a cash-secured put at a 3,000 strike.

      Pairs

      A call may list several quote/premium pairs; a put accepts exactly one pair whose quote token is the collateral. The premium token may be the underlying, the quote, or a third token.

      Physical or cash

      Physical at launch. Cash or Either require the optional cash feature enabled at creation; physical-only terms can never be loosened to cash.

      Every term, one by one

      The full list of terms, what activation checks for each, and what the owner may still change, is on the Vault terms shelf.

      LPs deposit collateral

      LPs add collateral and receive shares in proportion. Only the owner may deposit unless the vault was created with public deposits enabled.

      Add funds

      A deposit adds collateral and mints shares. Deposits must not be paused.

      Who may deposit

      Anyone other than the owner can deposit only if the vault was created with publicDeposits enabled. Vaults default to private deposits.

      Shares

      Shares determine an LP's part of the premium and of whatever remains at the end.

      Approve, then deposit

      Each depositor runs approve-token, then deposit with the same raw amount. Approvals target the clone, never the Hub.

      Take funds back

      Return some or all shares to withdraw the matching collateral. The contract burns those shares. This route exists only while Open.

      Owner may tighten the terms

      The owner may only strengthen LP protections, for example by raising the minimum premium. Loosening terms after LPs have committed is not possible.

      Tighten terms

      Raise the minimum premium, raise the strike limit for calls or lower it for puts, disable a pair, lower the permitted in-the-money distance. Never the other way.

      Hand over ownership

      Management of the vault can be transferred to another address. There is no dedicated CLI command; use the ABI.

      Owner opens or schedules the auction

      The owner opens the auction directly or sets a start time. Once that time is reached anyone may submit the opening transaction; nothing happens by itself.

      Open or schedule

      Open manually with openAuction, or set auctionStartsAt. Zero means only the owner may open.

      Conditions

      Admissions unpaused, a nonzero collateral balance at or above minCollateral, and expiry still in the future.

      Anyone may open on schedule

      Once the scheduled time is reached, anyone can submit the opening transaction. A schedule does not submit it automatically.
