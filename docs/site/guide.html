IVY · PROTOCOL ATLAS

Ivy Vaults

Deposit assets to back one option, earn its premium, and claim what remains when the option ends.

An Ivy vault is a smart contract for a single option trade. The buyer pays an upfront price, called the premium, for the right to trade an agreed asset at an agreed price, called the strike. Using that right is called exercising the option. The depositors provide the assets that back the trade, so what they receive at the end depends on whether the buyer exercises. Selling another option requires a new vault.

For example, an option on WETH might have a strike of 3,000 USDC per WETH. WETH is the underlying token, the asset the option is written on. USDC is the quote token, the currency used to express its price. A call gives the buyer the right to buy WETH at the strike; a put gives it the right to sell WETH at the strike.

With physical settlement, exercising exchanges those tokens. With cash settlement, the buyer receives the calculated payoff from the assets deposited in the vault instead. The guide follows the physical trade first, then explains the optional cash method.

Section 01

The people around a vault

An option trade has a seller and a buyer. In Ivy, liquidity providers, or LPs, supply the seller's backing assets, called collateral, and receive shares that record their stake in the vault. A market maker buys the option. The owner manages the vault, and the bid master selects the market maker's offer. The protocol administrator manages global settings and permissions, a different role from owning an individual vault. The roles below show who makes each decision and which contract holds the money.

OWNER

Sets the terms

The owner creates the vault and sets its tokens and trading terms. The owner may also be an LP and can fund the vault alone, accept deposits from other LPs, or do both.

LIQUIDITY PROVIDER

Supplies collateral

The LP deposits collateral and receives shares in one vault. Collateral is the asset set aside to meet the option's payment obligations. Shares record the LP's stake in that vault and determine its share of the premium and remaining assets.

MARKET MAKER

Buys and manages the option

The market maker is the option buyer and manages the position through settlement. It proposes the premium and option terms in a signed bid within the owner's limits. At activation, it pays the premium and holds the right to exercise under the agreed terms.

BID MASTER

Selects & submits

The bid master runs the auction off-chain, selects a buyer's signed bid and submits it to the Hub for activation.

Section 02

Vault lifecycle

A vault moves from Open to Auction, then Live and Settled. Cancelling an auction is the only way to return to an earlier phase. Once a bid activates, the vault can only move forward to Settled.

A vault does not fix its expiry at creation. The owner's bid rules say which expiries are acceptable, for example 10 to 30 days after activation, and the winning bid chooses the exact date and time. From activation on, that expiry is fixed. The exercise window is the period when the buyer may use its option. Its boundaries depend on the agreed rules, so expiry does not always mean exercise must stop immediately. The physical timing rules and cash timing rules below give the exact boundaries.

Who can act in each phase
PHASE 0
Open

No option has been sold yet. This is the stage for funding the vault and preparing the terms offered to buyers.

  • LPsTake funds back.An LP can return some or all of its shares to withdraw the corresponding collateral. The contract destroys those shares, called burning, because they no longer represent a stake in the vault. This withdrawal route is available only while Open.Add funds.A deposit adds collateral and gives the depositor shares. Deposits must not be paused. Anyone other than the owner can deposit only if the vault was created with publicDeposits enabled.
  • OwnerFix the bid rules.The owner chooses the rules a buyer's offer must pass when creating the vault, including the range of strikes and expiries it accepts. They cannot change afterwards, so LPs deposit against exactly what they read.Manage ownership.The owner can hand management of the vault to another address.

Moving to Auction. The owner can open the auction manually with openAuction or set a scheduled start time, auctionStartsAt. Once that nonzero time is reached, anyone can submit the opening transaction. A schedule does not submit it automatically.

Auction opening must not be paused. The vault needs a nonzero collateral balance that meets the configured minimum, minCollateral.

PHASE 1
Auction

The auction is open, and the trade terms are locked. The owner cannot change them while buyers are bidding.

  • LPsDeposits stop while bidding is active. LP withdrawals also wait unless the auction timeout or an admission pause makes the auction cancellable. If the admin enables transfers, shares remain transferable.
  • Bid masterCompare offers off-chain and select a buyer's signed bid. The offer must fit the vault's terms, and the buyer must have enough funds and token allowance to pay the premium.

Moving to Live. The bid master calls activate(vaultId, bid, marketMakerData, signature, bidMasterData). The two data arrays carry optional inputs for individual bid rules, one slot per rule. If the checks pass, the buyer pays the premium, the bid's strike and expiry become the option's, and the option begins.

When can the auction be cancelled? The bid master can cancel at any time. Once the auction timeout elapses, or admission is paused for this vault or across the protocol, anyone can cancel. The expiry rules do not unlock an auction: before activation there is no expiry. An LP can also withdraw directly once one of those public conditions holds.

By default the timeout is three days. The protocol admin can choose a different duration for new vaults; each vault copies the current setting at creation and keeps it. The clock starts at the first successful auction opening and cancellation plus reopening does not restart it.

What happens after cancellation? The vault returns to Open. Collateral stays in the vault until LPs choose to withdraw it; cancellation does not send funds back automatically. Deposits are possible again if they are not paused and the vault's deposit rules allow them.

The scheduled auction start is cleared. The owner can schedule another auction or open one manually. No premium or platform fee was collected during the cancelled auction, so neither needs to be refunded.

PHASE 2
Live

The option has been sold. Collateral is now committed to the buyer's remaining rights, so LPs must wait for settlement to claim the remaining collateral and proceeds.

  • Market makerThe buyer or its executor calls exercise(vaultId, amount) during the allowed exercise window. The amount is the quantity of the underlying token, such as WETH, that is covered by that exercise. It can be part or all of the remaining option size, depending on whether the vault's terms permit exercising in smaller portions.
  • LPsPremium claims are available immediately. Shares can be transferred when enabled by the admin.

Moving to Settled. Full exercise settles the vault in the exercise transaction. Otherwise, anyone can call settleAtExpiry(vaultId) once settling is allowed. Physical options must first finish their exercise window; cash options use a timely final expiry price or finish the fixed physical fallback window when that price is missing.

PHASE 3
Settled

The option is over, and no further exercise is possible. Final claims may contain a different mix of tokens from the original deposit, depending on how the option ended.

  • ShareholdersCall claim(vaultId, shares) to exchange shares for a proportional part of the remaining collateral and exercise proceeds. For example, a holder of 10% of the remaining shares can claim 10% of that available pool, subject to token rounding. The shares are burned when claimed. Reserved premium, platform fees and buyer payouts are excluded from this pool. A reserve is an amount set aside for a specific payment, so it is not available for this shareholder claim. To send all claimable tokens to another address, call claimTo(vaultId, shares, recipient, 7) from the address holding the shares. Your shares are burned; the chosen recipient receives the tokens. If a selected token transfer fails, the entire transaction reverts, including the share burn. You may select fewer tokens, but doing so permanently gives up the skipped token payout for the shares burned. See token selection and a worked example before using a mask other than 7.
  • Market makerThe buyer or its executor calls claimPayout(vaultId) if a payout is waiting in the buyer reserve. This is an owed cash-expiry payout for a cash-settled vault. The claim sends it to the configured recipient. Physical exercise already pays the recipient in the exercise transaction, so it creates no later buyer payout to claim.
Claims require transactions. Reaching Settled does not automatically distribute the remaining assets.

Selecting tokens with claimTo. Add the values for the tokens you want to receive: 1 for collateral, 2 for the premium token, and 4 for the token received through physical exercise. For calls, collateral is underlying and exercise proceeds are quote. For puts, these are reversed. 7 = 1 + 2 + 4 requests all three. Only unreserved balances are available; earned premium is claimed separately.

Suppose a call uses WETH collateral and USDC for both premium and quote. The shares you redeem entitle you to 2 WETH and 6,000 USDC. Mask 7 sends both amounts to your chosen recipient. If USDC cannot be transferred, mask 1 requests only the 2 WETH. On success, all the requested shares are burned and you permanently give up the 6,000 USDC for those shares. It stays in the vault for any remaining shares. If no shares remain, no LP can claim it through this function.

When roles share a token address, selecting any of them includes your share of that token's entire unreserved balance, paid once. To skip USDC in this example, omit both its premium and quote roles. Skipping a token does not defer payment: you cannot use burned shares to collect it later.

What a pause means. The guardian, an address with permission to pause new activity, can stop new commitments across the protocol or for one vault. This is called an admission pause. It blocks new creation where applicable, deposits, auction opening and activation. It does not stop withdrawals, transfers, exercise, settling at expiry or claims that otherwise meet their normal rules. A pause does not extend any deadline.

Section 03

Premium and fees

The premium is what the buyer pays to obtain the option. Imagine a buyer pays 1,000 USDC and the platform fee is 2%. The vault sets aside 20 USDC for the treasury, and the shareholders at activation earn the remaining 980 USDC. They can claim it immediately, without waiting to see whether the buyer exercises. Move the fee slider below to see how the split changes. The treasury is the address that receives the platform fee. Rates are also shown in basis points, abbreviated bps: 100 bps is 1%, so 200 bps is 2%.

Each vault fixes its fee rate at creation using the current global rate. Later rate increases or decreases affect only new vaults, including when an existing vault has a zero fee. Activation uses that fixed rate and records the current treasury. Anyone can then call claimPlatformFee() on the individual vault contract once to pay that recorded treasury.

Contracts count tokens in their smallest units, called raw units. For a token with six decimal places, 1 USDC is stored as 1,000,000 raw units. A calculation that lands between two raw units must round to one of them. In the formulas below, floor means round down and ceil means round up.

LPs collect unpaid premium by calling claimPremium(vaultId). Transferring shares also transfers a proportional amount of the sender's unpaid premium to the new holder. Premium already collected is not paid again. If an LP burns shares to claim settlement assets, any unpaid premium remains available for that LP to claim separately.

Calculation
floor(grossPremium × feeBps / 10,000) in premium tokens. The fee rounds down to a whole raw token unit, leaving the rounding remainder for LPs. The owner's PremiumMin rule's minPremiumPerUnit is the gross premium per whole underlying token, before the platform fee is deducted. The total payment scales that per-token premium by the option's size, called its notional. For example, 100 USDC per WETH over a 10 WETH option produces a 1,000 USDC gross premium.
Rate authority
The global fee rate starts at zero. An address with PLATFORM_FEE_MANAGER_ROLE can set it as high as 10,000 basis points, or 100%, for newly created vaults. Existing vaults keep their fixed rates. One basis point is 0.01%, so 200 bps means 2%. The deployment admin, the address chosen to administer the protocol when it is deployed, initially holds this permission and controls who else receives it.
Treasury authority
The deployment admin is the initial recipient. An address with DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE can change the recipient for future activations; later changes never rewrite recorded fees.
Earned payments
Once activation succeeds, exercise, expiry or a pause does not take back the premium and fees already earned. The vault tracks fees, LP premium and buyer obligations separately, even if they are all owed in the same token.

An LP's result depends on both the premium and the assets left after the option ends. With physical exercise, the buyer exchanges tokens with the vault at the agreed strike, so LPs may receive a different mix of tokens from their deposit. Section 05

Bidding and exercise

The auction happens off-chain. A buyer signs an offer, then the bid master submits it to activate the option and collect the premium. Signing alone does not open a position. Once activation succeeds, the buyer or its executor can exercise under the agreed terms.

  1. 01 · BUYER

    Sign the bid

    The buyer signs an offer specifying the price, premium and option rules for this auction. EIP-712 is the structured signing format that binds those fields into one message.

    MARKET_MAKER_ROLEThe buyer needs this role for activation.
  2. 02 · BUYER

    Approve the vault

    The buyer gives the individual vault permission to collect the full premium from its wallet. That amount is floor(premiumPerUnit × notional / underlyingUnit).

    Approval grants permission to spend; activation is the transaction that collects the premium. Exercise has its own payment requirements.
  3. 03 · BID MASTER

    Submit for activation

    Submit the signed bid with any per-rule data. The vault checks it and collects premium in the same transaction.

underlyingUnit = 10 ** underlyingDecimals is the number of raw units in one whole underlying token. Here ** means exponentiation: a token with 18 decimal places has 10 ** 18 raw units per whole token.

The premium is collected and the option becomes Live. The buyer can then exercise when its terms allow.

×

Any of those checks fail

The whole activation transaction is rejected. The vault stays in Auction and no premium is collected by that attempt.

Some bid rules take inputs at activation.

A rule's id is its position in rulesOf(vaultId). Activation carries two arrays, each empty or holding one slot per rule, and slot i reaches rule i only. Adding a rule never changes what another rule reads.

marketMakerData is the buyer's: the bid signs its hash as marketMakerDataHash, so the bid master cannot alter or drop it. A rule treats it as the buyer's claim, never as a market fact. bidMasterData is the bid master's, unsigned: an attestation such as a bid's implied volatility. A rule that reads it lets the bid master decide that check, so LPs trust the bid master for it. Both arrays are published in a RuleDataProvided event. Bid rules lists which kinds read these slots.

Exercise and settling at expiry

When the buyer exercises a physical option, it uses the right it bought: the caller pays one asset and the vault delivers the other at the agreed strike. If the buyer leaves some or all of that right unused, anyone can close it after the exercise window ends by calling settleAtExpiry. The two paths have different callers and payment requirements.

Physical exercise windows

American options may be exercised before expiry; European options must wait until expiry. Both physical styles remain exercisable for an additional period after expiry, called the grace window. This lasts 1 hour by default and is configured as exerciseWindow. The protocol admin chooses the setting for new vaults; each vault copies it at creation and keeps that duration. For example, with a one-hour window and an expiry of 12:00 UTC, exercise remains possible until just before 13:00 UTC. At 13:00 UTC it stops, and anyone can settle the vault. Section 06

Optional cash settlement

With cash settlement, the buyer receives the value of the option's payoff without exchanging the full underlying and quote amounts. That payoff, called intrinsic value, is calculated from the strike and a reported market price. Payment comes from the vault's collateral: a WETH-backed call pays WETH, even though the method is called cash settlement. This feature is implemented but disabled by default and requires an authorized price publisher.

Two separate admin controls

At launch, the protocol does not allow new cash-settled positions and has no authorized price publisher. The admin must separately enable new cash positions and give an address permission to submit their prices. Enabling one does not enable the other.

Allow new cash positions
Enabling cash admissions allows new vaults configured for Cash, meaning cash settlement only, or Either, meaning the buyer's bid may choose physical or cash settlement. It also allows cash bids to activate. Disabling admissions blocks those new commitments; existing Live positions still have to meet their payment obligations.
Authorize a price publisher
Granting the publisher role lets that address publish settlement prices. Revoking it stops future reports from that address; prices already published remain stored.

Before enabling cash positions, agree on the price methodology, evidence and operator. The publisher can be a regular wallet controlled by a private key, a multisig wallet requiring several signers, or a helper contract authorized to submit reports. The admin grants it SETTLEMENT_PRICE_PUBLISHER_ROLE, then calls setCashSettlementEnabled(true). Cash/Either vaults must set a positive maxSettlementPriceAge, the maximum age in seconds of a price used for exercise before expiry.

Publication route
A regular wallet submits a price transaction directly. A helper contract calls the same publication methods. In both cases, the report is written into the Hub; the Hub does not fetch the settlement price from a separate feed or helper. A helper must restrict who can submit prices through it; otherwise anyone could publish under its permission.
Publisher availability
Enabling cash does not check whether a publisher is ready or online. The admin is responsible for maintaining publication coverage.
When disabled
New Cash/Either vaults and cash bids, including pending auctions, are blocked. Physical bids may still activate, including in existing Either vaults.
Existing obligations
Physical vaults keep their delivery method. Existing cash positions retain publication, exercise, settling at expiry and claims; disabling removes none of their payment obligations.

Which price does the vault use?

Cash settlement needs a price report for this specific vault. Before expiry, an American option uses a recent reported market price, called an exercise observation. That report records both the price and when it was observed. At expiry, settlement switches to one final price for the option's original expiry time, even if the transaction happens much later.

Cash exercise, settling at expiry and claims

Exercising sends the buyer's payoff immediately. Settling at expiry instead sets aside the remaining payoff in a buyer reserve for a later claim. Both paths use the reported price, and the distinction determines when tokens leave the vault.

EXERCISE

Vault → recipient

Buyer or executor calls exercise(vaultId, amount). Intrinsic value is paid immediately, and a contract recipient is notified once the vault has finalized.

If the calculated payout is zero, exercise is rejected.

The vault's partial-exercise policy determines whether the buyer can exercise some of the remaining option or must exercise all of it. Full exercise settles the vault.

SETTLE AT EXPIRY

Vault → buyer reserve → recipient

At or after expiry, anyone calls settleAtExpiry(vaultId); buyer or executor later uses claimPayout(vaultId), which pays the recipient and notifies it if it is a contract.

Anyone can settle the vault, even when the buyer is owed nothing.

Settling closes the remaining option and sets aside any amount owed to the buyer. Shareholders can claim the assets left after that reservation. Earned premium stays separate.

With a timely final price, settling at expiry reserves remaining buyer value. Without one, the fixed physical fallback window applies; anyone can settle the vault afterward and the unexercised remainder lapses. Both outcomes need an on-chain transaction.

Missing expiry report: fixed physical fallback

Both American and European cash options use the same bounded recovery. Creation fixes the expiry price publication window P and the physical exercise window W. Publication closes at D = expiry + P; fallback closes at F = D + W. The buyer accepts publisher availability risk and must fund physical delivery if it chooses to exercise.

BLOCKED

Price-dependent settlement

Missing, stale or expired observations block early cash exercise without enabling early fallback. A missing final report blocks cash settlement during the publication window.

STILL AVAILABLE

Fixed route to LP claims

With no final price, explicit physical exercise is available from D inclusive to F exclusive. At F, anyone can settle the vault and LPs can claim, even if the buyer has done nothing. Premium claims remain available throughout.

Before D, an authorized replacement can publish the verified price for the original expiry. A timely final price remains valid after F and prevents fallback. At or after D, publication is rejected even without an earlier fallback transaction. Only the remaining notional enters fallback; completed exercises stay final.

The buyer or executor explicitly calls exercisePhysicalFallback(vaultId, amount). For calls it delivers quote at the strike and receives underlying; for puts it delivers underlying and receives quote at the strike. Approve the vault clone for the required token first. An ordinary cash exercise never switches to these transfers. Full exercise settles immediately; partial exercise never extends F. At F the unexercised remainder lapses without a new buyer reserve. No publisher, buyer callback or prior fallback activation is needed for expiration.

The publication and fallback deadlines are visible through settlementStatus. The original Cash type remains in history. P and W are disclosed before deposits and bidding and cannot be changed for an existing vault.

Recovery remains available under the cash admission rules and admission pause.

Section 08

Bid rules

What would you accept for selling this option? A strike between 3,100 and 3,500 USDC, at least 30 USDC per WETH, and an expiry 10 to 30 days away? Bid rules turn those choices into checks the Hub runs before a sale. The creator fixes the rule list at vault creation; the buyer's winning bid fixes the exact strike, premium and expiry.

One vault, three offers

Take a WETH call quoted and paid in USDC. Its creator lists the three required rules below. Assume each offer passes the Hub's signature, collateral and other mandatory checks. Days are measured from the moment the bid activates.

Now add StrikeSpotBand with 10% on either side of spot. If spot is 2,800 USDC, its ceiling is 3,080. Even the first offer fails, and no strike can satisfy both ranges. Each rule is valid on its own; creation does not check that their combination leaves a bid that can pass. Check the overlap before accepting deposits.

Every rule must say yes

Every vault needs StrikeRange, PremiumMin and at least one expiry rule: ExpiryTenor or ExpiryDates. The first two need one entry for every permitted pair. Add other rules when you need them; none replaces those required kinds.

Rules stack
The Hub checks the bid first, then runs every rule in list order. The first rejection reverts activation. Repeating a kind adds another check; listing both expiry kinds requires both to pass. For the shipped rules, reordering changes the first error, not the acceptable bids.
The list stays fixed
Each rule stores a validator address, a kind and configuration data. These are covered by the terms hash every bid signs. Changing them requires a new vault. Market-dependent rules still read the current feed; custom validators may depend on other changing state.
Validators need approval
Governance must grant the validator the Hub's bid-validator role before a new vault adopts it. The shipped IvyStandardBidRules offers the eight kinds below. Revoking the role blocks new adoption; existing vaults keep calling their stored validators.
Hub checks still apply
No rule can permit an unknown pair, zero strike, past expiry, disallowed exercise style or settlement type, a mismatched commitment, zero recipient, zero notional or a total premium that rounds to zero. Rules can reject a bid, but cannot change it, move tokens or write state.

Building a bid? See rule inputs at activation for the buyer's signed and bid master's unsigned data slots.

Make longer options cost more

A flat 30 USDC minimum accepts the same premium for 10 days as for 30. Add PremiumYieldFloor if you want the minimum to scale with time. With spot at 3,000 USDC and a 10% annual rate, a 36.5-day option needs 30 USDC per WETH; a 73-day option needs 60. Keep PremiumMin too. The bid must meet the higher of the two floors.

For one exact expiry, set both ExpiryDates endpoints to the same future timestamp. For a calendar window with at least seven days remaining, combine ExpiryDates with ExpiryTenor, for example 7 to 30 days. Both ranges apply at activation, so check their overlap as the auction approaches.

A valid rule is not a guaranteed sale

At creation, each validator checks its own configuration. The Hub rejects missing required kinds with MissingStrikeRange, MissingPremiumMin or MissingExpiryRule. Unapproved validators give InvalidValidator; an unsupported kind in the shipped validator gives UnknownRuleKind. These checks do not prove that all rules can pass together.

During the auction, a moving spot price can take an acceptable bid outside its band. A waiting bid's remaining tenor shrinks. If an ExpiryDates deadline passes, no bid can activate, but that deadline does not unlock deposits. LPs can exit after the auction timeout, during an admission pause, or after the bid master cancels. A failed activation leaves the auction open and transfers no premium.

After activation, the rules never run again. Exercise, settlement and claims use the winning bid's fixed terms.

Strike rules

Strikes are raw quote-token units per whole underlying token. A strike of 3,100 USDC is 3100000000, because USDC has six decimals. All range endpoints are inclusive. Basis points (bps) are hundredths of a percent: 1,000 bps is 10%.

StrikeRangeA fixed strike range · required

You set. One entry per pair: quoteToken, minStrike and maxStrike.

Passes when. minStrike ≤ strike ≤ maxStrike, both ends included, for calls and puts alike.

Example. With a range of 3,100 to 3,500 USDC, both 3,100 and 3,500 pass. One raw unit below 3,100 fails.

Only one side. For a floor only, set maxStrike to the largest uint256. For a ceiling only, set minStrike to 1.

Rejects with. StrikeBelowRange or StrikeAboveRange.

At creation. Exactly one entry for each pair the vault lists, and 0 < minStrike ≤ maxStrike. Otherwise RuleMissingPair, PairUnknown, DuplicatePair or InvalidStrikeRange.

StrikeSpotBandKeep the strike near spot · optional

You set. priceFeed, maxPriceAge in seconds, maxInTheMoneyBps and maxOutOfTheMoneyBps.

Passes when. For a call, spot × (1 − ITM) ≤ strike ≤ spot × (1 + OTM). For a put with OTM below 100%, spot × (1 − OTM) ≤ strike ≤ spot × (1 + ITM). ITM and OTM here are fractions: 1,000 bps is 0.10. Spot-band bounds round down to raw token units. Spot is the underlying's price in the bid's quote token.

Example. With spot at 3,000 USDC and both limits at 1,000 bps, a call may strike from 2,700 to 3,300.

Only one side. A call always has a finite upper bound. Even the largest OTM limit, 4,294,967,295 bps, still imposes a ceiling. A put with an OTM limit of 10,000 or more has no floor.

Rejects with. StrikeOutsideSpotBand. A zero or future-dated price gives InvalidPrice, and one older than maxPriceAge gives StalePrice.

At creation. The feed must be the release's IvyPriceFeed, maxPriceAge must be positive, and a call's ITM limit can be at most 10,000. Otherwise BindingMismatch, FeedNeedsMaxPriceAge or DeviationTooLarge, respectively.

Premium rules

Premium figures are the gross premium per whole underlying token, in the pair's premium token, before the platform fee. Flat floors do not grow with the option's length. PremiumYieldFloor scales the required premium with tenor. PremiumVolFloor checks an attestation; it does not calculate a premium.

PremiumMinA fixed premium minimum · required

You set. One entry per pair: quoteToken and minPremiumPerUnit, a flat amount such as 30 USDC per WETH.

Passes when. premiumPerUnit ≥ minPremiumPerUnit. No price feed is read.

Rejects with. PremiumTooLow.

At creation. Exactly one entry for each pair the vault lists, and a positive minimum. Otherwise RuleMissingPair, PairUnknown, DuplicatePair or PremiumTooLow.

PremiumSpotFloorPremium as a share of spot · optional

You set. priceFeed, maxPriceAge and minPremiumBps from 1 to 10,000.

Passes when. premiumPerUnit × 10,000 ≥ spot × minPremiumBps, with spot priced in the premium token.

Example. With spot at 3,000 USDC and 500 bps, the floor is 150 USDC per WETH.

No feed needed. When premium is paid in the underlying, spot is one whole token and the feed is never read. The feed may be the zero address only if every permitted pair pays premium in the underlying.

Rejects with. PremiumTooLow, InvalidPrice or StalePrice.

At creation. InvalidPremiumFloor for a rate outside 1 to 10,000. If any pair pays premium in another token, priceFeed must be the release feed and maxPriceAge must be positive, or BindingMismatch or FeedNeedsMaxPriceAge.

PremiumYieldFloorPremium scaled by tenor · optional

You set. priceFeed, maxPriceAge and minAprBps from 1 to 65,535.

Passes when. premiumPerUnit × 10,000 × 365 days ≥ spot × minAprBps × tenor. Tenor is the time from activation to the bid's expiry. The cross-product check avoids intermediate division; a fractional minimum needs the next whole raw token unit.

Example. With spot at 3,000 USDC and 1,000 bps, which is 10% a year, a 36.5-day option needs 30 USDC per WETH and a 73-day option needs 60.

No feed needed. The same as PremiumSpotFloor: premium paid in the underlying is priced without the feed.

Rejects with. PremiumTooLow, InvalidPrice or StalePrice.

At creation. InvalidPremiumFloor for a zero rate. The same feed and price-age requirements as PremiumSpotFloor apply.

PremiumVolFloorTrust the bid master’s volatility figure · optional

You set. minVolBps, annualized, where 10,000 is 100%.

Passes when. The bid master puts the bid's implied volatility in this rule's bidMasterData slot, as one ABI-encoded uint32 in bps, and it is at least minVolBps.

Trust. The rule ignores the buyer's own slot, so a buyer cannot vouch for its own price. You rely on the bid master's calculation. The contract does not check the figure against the premium, strike or expiry, and does not enforce a pricing model.

Rejects with. VolTooLow, or MissingAttestation when the slot is not exactly 32 bytes. A 32-byte value that is not a valid ABI-encoded uint32 fails decoding.

At creation. InvalidVolFloor for a zero minimum.

Expiry rules

The Hub only requires the expiry to be in the future. Your expiry rules set everything else. Durations are in seconds and dates are Unix timestamps.

ExpiryTenorTime from activation to expiry · choose this or ExpiryDates

You set. minTenor and maxTenor in seconds.

Passes when. The expiry falls between activation time plus minTenor and activation time plus maxTenor, both ends included.

Example. 10 to 30 days is 864,000 to 2,592,000. A minTenor of 0 accepts any future expiry up to the maximum.

Timing. The range is measured when the bid activates, not when the buyer signed it, so a waiting bid's remaining tenor shrinks.

Rejects with. ExpiryOutsideTenor.

At creation. maxTenor must be positive and at least minTenor, or InvalidExpiryTenor.

ExpiryDatesAbsolute expiry dates · choose this or ExpiryTenor

You set. notBefore and notAfter as Unix timestamps.

Passes when. notBefore ≤ expiry ≤ notAfter.

Example. Set both to the same time to require one exact future expiry.

Timing. If notAfter passes while the auction is open, no bid can pass. LPs can exit after the auction timeout, during an admission pause, or after the bid master cancels the auction.

Rejects with. ExpiryOutsideDates.

At creation. notBefore cannot be later than notAfter, and notAfter must be after the creation block's timestamp, or InvalidExpiryDates.

When the shipped rules are not enough

A new validator can add checks without a new Hub. Governance must review and approve it before creators can use it on new vaults. Existing vaults keep their stored rules. Treat a validator other than IvyStandardBidRules as custom code that depositors must trust.

A validator receives the signed bid, the vault's tokens and notional, its auction opening time, its own configuration, and one data slot each from the buyer and bid master. It can also read on-chain state. That could support Friday expiries, a minimum distance out of the money, a buyer allowlist, or signed oracle data that the validator verifies itself. These are possibilities, not shipped rule kinds.

A rule can only accept or reject activation. Changing how exercise, settlement or claims work requires a new Hub release.
