    Ivy Vaults

    A vault’s life

    Liquidity providers (LPs) back one option trade. The buyer pays a premium for the right to trade at an agreed price, the strike.

    Call: the right to buy the underlying asset.
    Put: the right to sell it.

    LP premium is available after activation.
    The remaining pool is available only after settlement.

    Select a topic to read it. Use + to grow a detail branch and − to fold it away. Several branches can stay open. Drag to pan and pinch or use the zoom controls to explore the grove.

    People and funds

    One vault backs one option trade. A new trade needs a new vault.

    Who makes the trade

    • Owner sets the terms.
    • LPs provide collateral.
    • Buyer pays for the option.
    • Bid master selects and submits the winning offer.

    Follow an instruction

    The Hub checks instructions and coordinates each separate vault.

    Choose a role to see its route. One person can hold more than one role.

    Who calls what

      Where the tokens live

      Each vault holds its own collateral and reserves.

      The shared Hub coordinates the trade without holding the tokens.

      Approve the vault

      Every deposit and payment is pulled from your wallet by the vault that holds the trade. Token approvals therefore target the individual vault, never the Hub.

      To deposit, approve the vault for its collateral token, then submit the deposit through the Hub.

      Pause new commitments

      An admission pause is a safety switch controlled by a guardian, an account authorized to pause new business.

      It blocks new vault creation, deposits, auction opening and activation. Existing exercise and claims keep their usual rules.

      Global or one vault

      A guardian can pause or unpause admission across the Hub or for one vault.

      Which routes stay available?

      These routes keep their usual rules:

      • Exercise and settling at expiry.
      • Premium and residual claims.
      • Permitted withdrawals.
      • Valid agreed unwinds.

      Deadlines do not move. A pause alone does not settle a Live vault.

      Open

      The option has not been sold. LPs can still add or withdraw collateral.

      Choose what to sell

      The owner defines the trade before deposits:

      • Tokens and exact expiry.
      • Exercise policy and settlement choices.
      • Limits a buyer’s offer must meet.

      Set the boundaries

      The owner fixes the trade’s boundaries before LPs fund it. Nothing economic changes afterwards.

      Complete parameter reference ↗

      What stays fixed

      These terms stay fixed:

      • Underlying and collateral tokens.
      • Exact option expiry.
      • Partial-exercise policy.
      • Premium-token choices.

      A buyer’s offer must use the same expiry.

      Strike and premium limits

      Limits protect LPs from a bad bid: no offer can win below the price they signed up for.

      • Call: may require a minimum strike.
      • Put: requires a nonzero maximum strike.

      Both may require a minimum premium per whole underlying token, paid in the pair’s premium token. These are the PairLimits rule.

      How bids are judged

      The owner lists bid rules at creation: each names a validator contract and its data. At activation the Hub runs its own checks first, then every rule in order. All must approve.

      • PairLimits: strike limit and premium floor per quote token.
      • SpotBand: strike within a band of the feed’s spot.
      • PremiumFloor: premium at least a share of spot.

      Rules from another validator are custom. An empty list accepts any well-formed bid.

      Optional activation price check

      A strike far into the money sells the option too cheaply. If the owner listed a SpotBand rule, activation also checks the strike against spot:

      • maxInTheMoneyBps limits the strike’s deviation into the money from spot.
      • maxPriceAge limits the age of that spot price.

      Both values are fixed at creation.

      Choose the backing

      Every option involves two tokens: the asset being optioned and the token that states its price. In a WETH/USDC option:

      • WETH is the underlying asset.
      • USDC is the quote token used to state the strike price.

      LPs back a covered call with WETH or a cash-secured put with USDC.

      Premium is the buyer’s payment for the option. It can use the underlying, quote or a third token.

      Premium is accounted for separately from the backing.

      Fix how the option settles

      Settlement is how the buyer gets value when it uses the option: by trading tokens at the strike, or by receiving the difference in value out of the collateral.

      The owner chooses a settlement policy:

      • Physical or Cash: fixes the method.
      • Either: the winning bid selects the method.

      Once activated, the buyer cannot switch methods.

      Physical exchange
      • Call: the buyer pays quote tokens and receives underlying.
      • Put: the buyer delivers underlying and receives quote tokens.

      Both use the agreed strike. No settlement price report is needed.

      When cash is available

      Cash-capable creation and activation require the administrator to enable cash. It is disabled by default.

      Cash terms also need a positive observation-age limit.

      LPs fund the vault

      Deposits mint shares in this vault.

      While Open, an LP can burn shares to withdraw collateral. Opening the auction closes that exit.

      Deposit for shares

      The deposit mints shares representing that LP’s stake in this vault.

      For example, contributing 2 WETH of a final 10 WETH pool gives an LP 20% of its shares.

      Those shares determine its portion of the net premium and, later, the remaining pool.

      Approve the vault before depositing →

      Who may deposit?
      • Owner: may deposit.
      • Other LPs: may deposit only if publicDeposits was enabled.

      The vault must be Open and admission unpaused.

      Withdraw before the lock

      While Open, an LP can take some or all of its collateral back.

      After the auction opens, this withdrawal route is unavailable unless the auction is cancelled.

      What withdrawal burns

      Withdrawing while Open burns the corresponding vault shares and returns collateral. After cancellation, LPs use this same withdrawal route; the cancellation itself sends no tokens.

      Lock the funded pool

      Opening the auction commits the funded pool to a search for a buyer.

      Until an opening transaction succeeds, LPs retain their Open-phase withdrawal route.

      Open the auction

      The owner may open the auction directly or set a public start time.

      See the auction →

      How much is enough?

      The pool must be nonzero and meet the owner’s minCollateral threshold. The opening transaction must execute before the fixed option expiry.

      A schedule needs a transaction

      Scheduling only sets a time. auctionStartsAt is the earliest the opening transaction may run.

      • From that time: anyone may submit the opening transaction.
      • Before that time: the owner may open sooner.

      Time passing does not open the auction automatically.

      What opening locks

      Opening stops:

      • Deposits.
      • Open-phase withdrawals.
      • Changes to terms.

      The Hub records the actual opening time and a new auction ID.

      Cancellation returns the vault to Open and clears the schedule.

      Auction

      The collateral is committed, but the option has not yet been sold.

      Buyers offer a premium

      Bidding happens off-chain.

      Each signed offer is tied to this vault, auction and share supply, and must fit the owner’s limits.

      Sign and fund the offer

      The buyer signs an off-chain offer; the bid master submits it.

      Signing alone does not sell the option. Payment readiness is checked when the offer is activated.

      Signed-bid format and validation ↗

      The proposed trade

      An offer is a complete trade, not just a price. The signed offer specifies:

      • Price and payment: quote token, strike and premium.
      • Exercise: American or European style.
      • Settlement: physical or cash.
      • Expiry: the exact option expiry.

      All must fit the owner’s terms.

      The specific auction

      An offer is for one auction and the pool as it stands, so it cannot be replayed elsewhere. The signature binds:

      • Vault ID
      • Auction ID
      • Collateral share supply
      • Pair-terms hash

      Cancelling and reopening creates a new auction ID. The old bid no longer matches.

      A separate signature deadline

      Signing is free and off-chain, so a buyer needs a way to stop an old offer from being accepted later.

      validUntil is the last time the signed bid can be accepted. It is separate from the option expiry and the auction cancellation timeout.

      The buyer can also cancel its nonce to invalidate the bid at any time. The nonce also prevents reuse.

      Who pays the premium?

      The signed market maker is the option buyer and must have the required role.

      Its wallet must hold the premium and approve the individual vault to collect it.

      An executor or recipient may be named separately.

      Collateral stays committed

      LPs cannot deposit or withdraw during Auction.

      Who enables transfers?

      Shares are transferable only when the protocol administrator enables transfers.

      Before transferring, check the effects on:

      • Unclaimed premium →
      • Unwind approvals →

      Activate the winning offer

      Activation validates the selected offer and collects the premium in one transaction.

      A failed check leaves the vault in Auction.

      Accept the winning bid

      The bid master submits the buyer’s signed offer before expiry.

      Success fixes the option terms and makes the buyer’s exercise right live.

      Follow the live option →

      What must pass?

      Activation is the one moment the offer is verified. Everything must line up, or the vault stays in Auction:

      • Authorization: the buyer’s signature and nonce.
      • Auction: identity and current share supply.
      • Timing: bid deadline and fixed option expiry.
      • Terms: the owner’s allowed limits and choices.

      Admission must be unpaused and the premium transfer must succeed.

      Where the premium goes

      The premium enters the individual vault.

      • Treasury: receives the share determined by the saved platform-fee rate.
      • LPs: are credited with the remainder.

      Backing collateral stays committed for exercise.

      Cancel an unsold auction

      Cancellation returns the vault to Open with its collateral inside and clears the scheduled start.

      LPs can then withdraw, or a new auction can be opened before expiry.

      Return to Open

      The bid master can cancel any unsold auction. The owner can cancel once any one of the three conditions below is met.

      Return to funding →

      After the auction timeout

      The timeout keeps a pool from staying locked in an auction nobody wins. The owner can cancel once this deadline is reached:

      auctionOpenedAt
      + auctionTimeout

      The clock starts when the opening transaction succeeds, not when the auction is scheduled.

      Which duration applies?

      auctionTimeout is the administrator’s setting copied into this vault at creation. Later changes to the Hub setting do not change this vault’s duration.

      A concrete clock

      For an illustrative two-hour timeout:

      • At 10:00, the auction opens.
      • At 12:00, the owner can cancel.

      Reopening starts a new clock. The saved duration stays the same.

      At the option’s expiry

      The owner can cancel at or after the fixed option expiry, even if the auction timeout has not elapsed.

      Returning to Open lets LPs withdraw. An expired option cannot be auctioned again.

      During an admission pause

      An admission pause stops new commitments: vault creation, deposits, auction opening and activation. A guardian controls this safety switch.

      If the pause applies to this auction, the owner may cancel immediately. The pause can cover:

      • Globally across the Hub.
      • For this vault alone.

      No auction cancellation timeout wait is required. Cancellation returns the unsold vault to Open so LPs can withdraw.

      How the pause works and which rights remain →

      Live

      The buyer has paid. The backing stays committed until the option settles.

      LPs can claim premium

      The option is now Live. LPs can collect their earned payment while the collateral continues to back the trade.

      Collect the earned payment

      LPs can claim net premium as soon as activation succeeds. They keep it whether the option is exercised, expires or is unwound.

      Gross premium to net premium

      An illustrative 1,000 USDC premium at a 2% platform fee leaves:

      • LPs: 980 USDC.
      • Treasury: 20 USDC.

      Each vault saves its fee rate at creation.

      An LP’s share

      An LP with 20% of the shares receives 20% of the net premium. In the 980 USDC example, that is 196 USDC, separate from its later residual claim.

      After a transfer or burn

      Premium is credited to shares, not to wallets. So:

      • Transferred shares: unclaimed premium entitlement follows them.
      • Burned shares: unpaid premium credit is preserved.
      • Already claimed: premium is never paid again.

      Buyer uses the option

      The buyer decides whether to use its right to trade. It may act directly or delegate execution.

      The exercise style determines when that right can be used.

      Know the physical deadline

      A physical option does not close at the exact expiry. The vault gives the buyer a saved grace period, the exerciseWindow, to pay and take delivery after expiry.

      Physical exercise must execute strictly before expiry + exerciseWindow. This applies to both American and European styles.

      American starts at activation

      The buyer or executor may exercise from activation until just before the physical exercise deadline.

      European starts at expiry

      The buyer or executor must wait until the fixed option expiry. Exercise then remains available until just before the same physical deadline.

      Which exercise window?

      exerciseWindow is the Hub setting copied into the vault at creation. Later administrator changes affect new vaults, not this saved duration.

      Expiry at noon, one-hour window

      With an illustrative one-hour window:

      • 12:00 expiry: physical exercise remains available.
      • 13:00 cutoff: physical exercise stops; anyone may settle the vault.

      No price report is needed.

      Exchange tokens physically

      Physical exercise is the buyer using the option: it trades at the agreed strike, whatever the market price is now.

      For 10 WETH at a 3,000 USDC strike, full physical exercise means:

      • Call: the buyer pays 30,000 USDC and receives 10 WETH.
      • Put: the buyer delivers 10 WETH and receives 30,000 USDC.

      If partial exercise is enabled, exercising 2 of the 10 WETH leaves the other 8 Live.

      Exercising all remaining notional settles the vault.

      The premium is a separate payment and does not change with exercise.

      After full exercise →

      When is partial exercise allowed?

      A buyer may want to use only part of the option. Whether that is allowed is fixed by the owner at creation, as allowPartialExercise:

      • False: exercise must use all remaining notional.
      • True: exercise may use part and leave the rest Live.

      Delegate execution if needed

      The buyer can configure an executor and a recipient separately.

      Permission to submit a transaction and the destination of its assets are two different choices.

      Executor is permission to act

      The buyer or its configured executor may exercise and claim payouts.

      Only the buyer may change the executor or recipient.

      Delegation does not transfer ownership of the option.

      Where the buyer receives assets

      The buyer names a recipient in its signed offer: its own wallet or another address. Only the buyer can change this destination.

      What goes to that address?

      • Physical exercise: underlying tokens for a call; quote tokens for a put.
      • Cash exercise: the payout in the collateral token.
      • After settlement: any reserved cash payout or agreed unwind refund, once the buyer or executor claims it.

      Who supplies the tokens? The buyer or executor submitting a physical exercise must hold the input tokens and approve this vault to spend them.

      Example: an executor pays USDC from its wallet; the physical call sends WETH to the chosen recipient.

      A contract recipient is told it was paid

      A recipient that is a contract, such as a swap adapter, needs to know it was paid. So after each payout the contract calls onIvyPayout(vaultId, token, amount) on any recipient with code.

      The tokens are already delivered when the call runs, and the vault is already settled if that payout completed it.

      A recipient without that method, or one that reverts, never blocks the payout. Only a recipient that runs out of gas reverts the transaction, so gas estimates stay honest.

      Example: a recipient contract receives WETH from a cash exercise and swaps it to USDC in the same transaction, or posts an order for a solver to fill later.

      Cash uses a reported price

      Cash settlement pays the option’s intrinsic value: the value of a favorable difference between the reported market price and the agreed strike.

      An authorized price publisher records the price for this vault. The contract uses that report to calculate the payout.

      Example: a put on 1 WETH with a 3,000 USDC strike and a reported price of 2,700 USDC pays 300 USDC.

      Payment uses the collateral token: WETH for a WETH-backed call, USDC for a USDC-backed put. “Cash” does not always mean a stablecoin.

      Check whether cash is enabled →

      Exercise: request payment

      To exercise a cash option is to request its payout from the vault.

      Example: a cash put on 1 WETH has an agreed price, or strike, of 3,000 USDC.

      1. A protocol-authorized account, the price publisher, reports 1 WETH = 2,700 USDC.
      2. The buyer or executor submits the exercise transaction.
      3. The contract pays 3,000 − 2,700 = 300 USDC from the vault to the buyer’s chosen recipient.

      The buyer or executor does not send WETH to receive this cash payout.

      Who supplies the market price?

      A price publisher is an account or contract with permission to submit prices to the Hub. The protocol’s administrator grants that permission.

      The publisher records a price report for this vault. For example, “1 WETH = 2,700 USDC.”

      The vault reads that stored report. It does not fetch a price from an exchange when the buyer exercises, and the buyer’s exercise request cannot set the price.

      The publisher supplies the market price; the contract calculates the payout from that price and the option’s agreed terms.

      Which report supplies that price?

      The option’s expiry time determines which report the contract reads:

      • Before expiry: a recent market-price report. Only American options can exercise this early.
      • At or after expiry: the final price reported for the expiry time. This applies to American and European options.

      Example: expiry is 16:00 and its final price is 2,700 USDC per WETH. Exercising at 16:10 still uses 2,700, even if the market has since moved to 2,500.

      How recent must the report be?

      Before expiry, an American cash option uses the latest report stored for this vault. The report must pass two checks when exercise executes:

      • The price was observed recently enough: within the vault’s maxSettlementPriceAge.
      • The report’s own deadline, validUntil, has not passed.

      Example: the price was observed at 12:00, the maximum age is 5 minutes, and the report expires at 12:03.

      Exercise at 12:02 can use it. At 12:04, its deadline has passed, so a new valid report is needed.

      Why doesn’t the price keep changing?

      The final expiry price fixes the market price used to value the option at its agreed expiry time.

      Example: for the put on 1 WETH with a 3,000 USDC strike, a final price of 2,700 gives a 300 USDC payout.

      If the market falls to 2,500 later, the payout remains 300 USDC. Waiting does not turn it into 500 USDC.

      The authorized publisher can record this price from expiry until strictly before expiry + expiryPricePublicationWindow. Once recorded, it cannot be replaced or expire. Without a timely price, the fixed physical fallback below applies.

      Why can exercise happen after expiry?

      For a cash option, expiry fixes the time used to value the option. It does not automatically pay anyone or close the vault.

      At or after expiry, the buyer or executor can exercise both American and European cash options while the vault is Live and the final expiry price is available.

      Anyone can also settle the vault at expiry. That values the rest of the option at the same final price and sets the buyer’s payout aside in the vault for a separate claim. The buyer gets the same amount either way.

      Once the vault is Settled, the buyer claims its reserved payout instead of exercising.

      How settling at expiry closes the vault →

      How the collateral is divided

      The buyer’s payoff comes out of the vault’s collateral. The LPs share what remains when the vault settles.

      • A call has a payoff when the reported price is above the strike.
      • A put has a payoff when the reported price is below the strike.

      Exercise transfers the payoff to the buyer’s recipient immediately. Settling at expiry sets it aside in the vault for a separate claim.

      The examples below assume no earlier exercise. Premium is a separate payment and is not subtracted from these payoff amounts.

      Call example: a payout in WETH

      With no earlier exercise, the vault holds 10 WETH. The strike is 3,000 USDC per WETH; the final price is 3,300.

      • The favorable price difference is 300 USDC per WETH.
      • For 10 WETH, that gives 3,000 USDC of value.
      • The vault pays in WETH: 3,000 ÷ 3,300 ≈ 0.9091 WETH.

      After settling at expiry, about 0.9091 WETH is reserved for the buyer and 9.0909 WETH remains for LPs. Both stay in the vault until claimed.

      These figures are rounded for display. The contract rounds the buyer’s payout down to the token’s smallest unit.

      Put example: a payout in USDC

      A put covers 10 WETH at a 3,000 USDC strike. With no earlier exercise, the vault holds 30,000 USDC of collateral.

      If the final price is 2,700 USDC per WETH, the favorable difference is 300 USDC per WETH.

      • Buyer reserve: 10 × 300 = 3,000 USDC.
      • LP remainder: 30,000 − 3,000 = 27,000 USDC.

      Settling at expiry sets aside the buyer’s reserve. The buyer or executor claims it for the recipient; LPs claim the remainder according to their shares.

      When the buyer’s payoff is zero

      A call pays zero at or below its strike. A put pays zero at or above its strike.

      Example: a call backed by 10 WETH has a 3,000 USDC strike. If the final price is 2,800, settling at expiry reserves nothing for the buyer. All 10 WETH remains for LP claims, assuming no earlier exercise.

      The premium remains earned. A zero-payoff exercise is rejected, so settling at expiry closes the vault instead.

      If the cash price is missing

      The contract needs a valid price to calculate what the buyer is owed. A missing price is not a zero payoff.

      Early cash exercise needs a valid report. At expiry, the vault waits through its fixed expiry price publication window; without a price, a fixed physical exercise window follows.

      Once both windows end, anyone can settle the vault and LPs can claim. No buyer funding or publisher recovery is needed for that transaction.

      The recovery steps differ before and after expiry.

      Missing prices and recovery ↗

      A missing price before expiry

      American cash exercise needs a fresh observation. A missing, too-old or expired report blocks that exercise.

      Example: the maximum observation age is 5 minutes. At 12:06, a price observed at 12:00 is too old, even if its report deadline has not passed.

      An authorized publisher can record a newer valid observation. The buyer or executor can then retry exercise while it is still before expiry.

      Once expiry arrives, the vault needs the final expiry price instead. European cash options cannot exercise before expiry at all.

      A missing price after expiry

      Define D = expiry + P and F = D + W. P is the saved expiry price publication window; W is the saved exercise window. A recent pre-expiry observation cannot replace a final price.

      If a final price lands before D, normal cash settlement remains available even after F. Otherwise D ≤ now < F permits explicit physical exercise of the remaining notional, for both American and European options.

      Calls pay quote at the strike to receive underlying; puts deliver underlying to receive quote. The buyer or executor approves the clone and explicitly calls exercisePhysicalFallback. Ordinary cash exercise never pulls these assets.

      At F, anyone can settle the vault without a prior fallback transaction. Unexercised notional lapses, opening LP claims without a new cash reserve. Settings or publisher changes never extend either deadline.

      Close the remaining position

      A Live vault reaches Settled through:

      • Full exercise.
      • Settling at expiry.
      • An executed agreement between the buyer and every current shareholder.

      Settle at expiry

      settleAtExpiry is the transaction that closes an option the buyer did not fully use.

      Anyone may call it once the relevant condition holds:

      • Physical option: its physical exercise window has closed.
      • Cash option: expiry has arrived with a timely final price, or the fixed fallback deadline has arrived without one.

      For a cash option, it reserves any remaining intrinsic payoff for the buyer’s separate claim. The unreserved remainder opens to LP claims.

      Time passing alone never settles a vault.

      See settlement and claims →

      Physical: when exercise closes

      The saved exerciseWindow is the extra time after option expiry. This example uses one hour.

      Exercise must execute strictly before 13:00. Settling is allowed from 13:00 exactly.

      The vault must still be Live. Full exercise can settle it earlier; time passing alone does not settle it.

      Cash: publication, fallback, settlement

      American and European cash options use the same fixed deadlines. These one-hour windows are illustrative.

      The vault must still be Live. Publishing the price does not settle it; full exercise or settling at expiry does.

      When can you claim as an LP?

      Physical: if the option has not been fully exercised, wait until 13:00 in this example. You or anyone else can then settle the vault.

      Cash: from expiry with a timely final price, or at the fallback deadline if none arrived. Anyone can settle it; only the timely-price route creates a new cash payout reserve.

      After the vault becomes Settled, you can claim your share of the unreserved assets. You do not need to wait for the option holder to claim its reserve.

      Agree to end early

      A Live option normally ends by exercise or by settling at expiry. An unwind ends it sooner: the buyer gives the option up, and current LPs pay the buyer an agreed refund in premium tokens.

      It only happens if the buyer and every current shareholder agree. Four steps: the buyer signs a proposal, LPs approve it, LPs fund the refund, anyone executes.

      Agreement, funding and recovery ↗

      After the agreement executes →

      Step 1: the buyer signs a proposal

      The deal starts as a proposal the buyer signs. It names the refund the buyer expects and a deadline to execute by.

      The signature also locks in the position as it is now: the vault, a proposal nonce, how much has been exercised and how many shares exist. If any of that changes, the signature no longer matches.

      The owner or buyer submits it. Changing anything means a new buyer signature.

      Step 2: every LP approves and funds its share

      The refund comes from the LPs, not from the vault. So every current shareholder does two things: approve the proposal, and deposit its share of the refund in premium tokens.

      For a 100 USDC refund with 60% / 40% ownership:

      • 60% holder: 60 USDC.
      • 40% holder: 40 USDC.

      Premium already earned and platform fees stay where they are.

      Step 3: anyone executes before the deadline

      Once the buyer has signed, every LP has approved and the refund is fully funded, the agreement is ready. Anyone may execute it while the vault is still Live and the deadline has not passed.

      Execution settles the vault: the option ends, the refund is reserved for the buyer to claim, and LPs claim the remaining collateral as after any settlement.

      What breaks an agreement before it executes

      Consent and funding are checked at execution, not when they were given. Between proposal and execution:

      • A share transfer cancels both the sender’s and the recipient’s approvals.
      • Further exercise changes the signed position, so the signature no longer matches.
      • The deadline passes.

      Proposing does not pause anything: the buyer can still exercise, and the vault can still be settled at expiry.

      Get an unused contribution back

      Funding is a deposit, not a payment. Until execution it is still the LP’s money.

      • Before execution: withdraw it at any time. Withdrawing also withdraws your approval. This includes after the deadline passes or the proposal is replaced.
      • After execution: the agreed refund belongs to the buyer. Only anything paid above your required share comes back.

      Settled

      The option has ended. Remaining assets stay in the vault until claimed.

      LPs collect the remainder

      Settlement opens the final collateral exit. The assets left for LPs depend on how the option ended.

      Redeem the remaining pool

      Current shareholders burn shares to receive the same proportion of the unreserved assets. Holding 20% of remaining shares means receiving 20% of the remaining pool.

      What a call leaves
      • No exercise: the pool holds underlying.
      • Full physical exercise: the pool holds quote tokens paid by the buyer.
      • Partial physical exercise: the pool can hold both.
      What a put leaves
      • No exercise: the pool holds quote collateral.
      • Physical exercise: underlying comes in from the buyer; quote goes out.
      • Partial exercise: the pool can hold both.
      What LP claims exclude

      These payments stay protected:

      • Unpaid premium.
      • Treasury obligations.
      • Buyer payouts or refunds.

      An LP residual claim cannot spend those reserves.

      Buyer collects reserved assets

      A settled vault may still owe the buyer:

      • Cash settlement at expiry: a payoff in collateral tokens.
      • Executed unwind: a refund in premium tokens.

      These reserves are separate from assets already delivered during exercise.

      Claim the reserved payout

      The buyer or its configured executor submits the claim. Reserved assets go to the configured recipient.

      Settlement does not send this payment automatically.

      A zero payoff creates no buyer reserve to claim.
